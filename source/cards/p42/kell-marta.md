## Draw

Forward-settled from 2011, retrieved knowing the shape of the next eighty
years and telling nobody — a discipline she enforced on herself before Iron
Bell ever thought to ask it of her. States, plainly, the position nobody
else at the camp will say out loud: the loop is already closed, so nothing
here was ever a decision, only a record being filled in on schedule.

Everyone has stopped trusting her, and for the one reason that should make
her trustworthy — she is always right, and being right is the only thing
she has left of home.

## Story

### THE PART SHE ALREADY KNOWS

Corporal Deakins wants to know whether to put his name down for the November
rotation or wait for February, and he has come to Kell about it the way
half the enlisted men eventually do, not because she has ever given anyone
useful advice but because she has never once been wrong about anything she
was willing to answer, which the camp has confused, understandably, for the
same thing as wisdom.

Kell knows which rotation he should take. She has known it, in outline,
since before she met him, filed somewhere in the part of her memory she
does not examine on purpose, the way a person does not examine the exact
date of their own death even when they happen to have read it. November
costs him a shoulder and February costs him nothing at all, and the
difference between those two sentences is the entire content of the advice
he is asking for, and she is not going to give it to him, because the rule
she made for herself in 2011 — spoken to no one, enforced before anyone at
this camp thought to enforce it on her — was that the record does not
become less true for her having warned somebody. It only becomes a record
with her handwriting on it too.

"I can't tell you that," she says, which is what she always says, and
watches him take it as the shrug of a woman who does not care, which is
easier for both of them than the truth: that the loop was closed before he
asked and will still be closed however she answers, and her silence
changes nothing about November except who carries the knowledge of it
beforehand.

He thanks her anyway, the way people thank an oracle for declining to
speak, and goes back to his bunk to think it over, as though thinking will
do the work of a decision that, by her own accounting, was never really
his to make, and Kell sits with the cold coffee and the arithmetic she was
doing on the back of somebody else's form, and finds, for the length of
one cigarette, that she minds this one more than she usually lets herself
mind any of them — not because Deakins matters more than the others, and
she is careful afterward to make sure that this is actually true and not a
kindness she is doing herself — but because for once somebody had wanted,
openly, to be told that none of it was his fault, and she is the only
person at Iron Bell who could have told him that honestly, and the rule
she made for herself does not have an exception for the truths that would
only have helped.

She puts down November in her own book, under his name, and does not tell
him that either.
