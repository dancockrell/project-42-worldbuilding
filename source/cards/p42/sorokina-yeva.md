## Draw

Lost somewhere in the Russian civil war in 1919, one name among several
million the record never sorted out, which taught her, before she was
twenty, the exact administrative weight of a list with your name spelled
wrong on it — a lesson Camp Iron Bell has done nothing to unteach her.

She breaks intercepted traffic faster than anyone who has timed her, and
trusts exactly one person on the installation: Mrs Ida Prentiss, who has
no clearance, no rank, and therefore nothing to gain from being told the
truth.

## Story

### THE FOURTH COLUMN

The discrepancy was eleven minutes, which was nothing, which was the kind
of nothing Sorokina had trained herself, at nineteen, in a filing office
in Kharkov that no longer existed under that name or possibly at all, to
treat as the beginning of something rather than the end of it, because
eleven minutes on a coherence form was either a clerk's tired hand or the
first quiet edit in a file that would, by the third or fourth pass, no
longer describe the same man.

She did not take it to Vosburgh. She had watched, in another country and
another alphabet, what happened to people who took a discrepancy to the
man whose desk the discrepancy had originated from, on the theory that he
would want to know, and had learned that a man in that position wants
several things more urgently than he wants to know, and that finding out
which of those things he wants that week is not a bet she has ever been
willing to place twice.

She took it instead to the mess hall, at an hour when Prentiss was known
to eat alone with a ledger of her own, and set the two timestamps in front
of her without comment, because Prentiss had a gift, rare at this
installation and rarer everywhere else Sorokina had lived, for looking at
a fact and asking only what the fact was, rather than what having been
shown it made her.

Prentiss looked at the eleven minutes for a while, and said she did not
know what it meant, which Sorokina had expected and had come to value
almost as much as an answer, because Prentiss was the only person at Iron
Bell with the standing to guess and the discipline not to, having neither
the clearance that would make a guess an accusation nor the rank that
would make it an order.

"Keep the paper," Prentiss said, which was not advice and was not a
directive and committed nobody to anything, and was, Sorokina had come to
understand over eleven months of similar exchanges, the single most
useful sentence available at this camp, because it asked nothing of the
person receiving it beyond continuing to notice what she had already
noticed.

Sorokina went back to the cipher desk and did not report the eleven
minutes to anyone with the authority to act on it, and started a fourth
column in a different pencil, dated, in case the file it belonged to was
ever the kind of file somebody needed a witness for later, and pushed it
to the edge of the blotter where it would not be mistaken for finished
work, and went back to the traffic she was actually paid to break.
