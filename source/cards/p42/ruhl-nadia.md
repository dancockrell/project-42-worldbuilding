## Draw

Pulled from the Aire in November 1926, the night the Kirkstall ferry went
over — one name lifted out of a form that needed only one, because the
programme's own criteria file records her loss as "qualifying, with
multiple qualifying candidates," a sentence she was not shown until she
asked for it in her own hand.

She does not resent being the one taken. She resents that a sentence like
that could exist at all, filed and initialed, deciding nothing on purpose
and everything by accident.

## Story

### MULTIPLE QUALIFYING CANDIDATES

Falk had told her, twice, that requesting her own criteria file was not
forbidden exactly, only unusual, and unusual at Camp Iron Bell tended to
mean something before anyone had decided what, and Ruhl had signed the
request form anyway, in the particular hand of somebody who has decided a
thing will happen and is now simply completing the paperwork that agrees
with her.

The folder came back in nine days, thinner than she had expected a
decision about her own life to be, four pages of the same flat
typescript the whole camp ran on, and she found the sentence on the second
page, in the middle of a paragraph that had clearly been written by
somebody thinking about something else — *subject's absence consistent
with surviving record; qualifying, with multiple qualifying candidates* —
and she read it three times before she understood that the third word from
the end was doing all the work in the sentence, and that the work it was
doing was telling her, in the driest possible register, that she had not
been chosen from everyone. She had been chosen from a smaller number of
people who had also drowned that night in the Aire, whose names the form
did not carry because the form had no column for a name it had not needed.

She had spent two years at Iron Bell being grateful, in the private way
that does not require anyone else's permission, and grateful was, she now
understood, the wrong shape entirely for what had happened to her. Nobody
had looked at a river full of the recently dead and picked the one worth
keeping. Somebody — she could not even locate a somebody, the sentence was
passive in exactly the place a name should have gone — had run a test
against a record, and the test had returned more than one answer, and
whichever hand initialed the box had taken the first one, or the nearest
one, or the one whose paperwork happened to be on top, and that was the
entire mechanism standing behind the fact of her own continued existence.

She did not tell Falk what the sentence said, when Falk asked, gently,
whether the file had been worth requesting. She said it had answered her
question, which was true, and let Falk read into that whatever comfort
Falk needed to read into it, because the actual answer — that there had
been at least one other person in that river whom the same test would have
passed just as cleanly, and that the only reason Ruhl was standing in a
records office in Mississippi and that person was not was a coin that
nobody had thought to call a coin — was not a thing she had found a way to
say out loud yet, to Falk or to anyone, and was not, she suspected, a thing
that got easier to say the longer she carried it alone.

She put the folder back in its drawer herself, rather than return it to
the clerk, and did not request anything else that year.
