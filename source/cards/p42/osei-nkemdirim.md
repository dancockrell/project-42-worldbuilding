## Story

### THE CARD IN THE BACK

The volume was exactly where the accession number said it would be, on the
third shelf from the door, between a manual on wireless telegraphy and a
history of the county that nobody had opened since it was donated, and Osei
found it in his second week, not looking for it, looking instead for
something to read that was not a requisition or a report, and he had
carried it back to the Annex and sat with it for the length of an evening
before he understood what he was holding, which was two papers he had
written in 1909, in a hand he no longer used, under a name that had been
his supervisor's the entire time and had, evidently, gone on being his
supervisor's for thirty-five years after the man who bore it had stopped
needing anything from anybody, cholera having settled that particular
account in 1911 along with several thousand other accounts in the same
month.

He did not say anything about this to anyone, there being, as far as he
could determine, nothing to say that would not require explaining a
grievance to people who could not act on it, the arrangement having been
his own idea at the time, made because a paper under an Englishman's name
travelled and a paper under his own did not, and a young man wanting his
mathematics to be read had made, he still believed, the only sensible
choice available to him, which did not stop the choice from having cost
exactly what it cost, thirty-five years later, in a library in Mississippi,
in a country that would not have let him publish under his own name here
either.

He requested a transfer to the Line Shed the following month, where the
oil and the copper made a noise loud enough to think inside without the
thinking showing on his face, and the request was denied on the grounds
that he was too valuable where he already was, which he understood to be
true, and which he also understood to be the precise mechanism by which a
man's usefulness becomes the wall of his own room — that Wexford's high
regard for him, sincerely held and probably correct, was functioning,
without either of them intending it, as the reason he would go on sitting
at the same desk doing the same closure work for as long as the programme
required it, which was to say indefinitely, there being no form for
releasing an asset merely because the asset had once, decades ago and
under conditions nobody at this installation could have prevented, been
denied his own name.

He has not resubmitted the request. He has not gone back to the library
either, though the volume is still there and the card in its pocket has
several lines on it now, filled in by hands he does not recognise, and he
finds, when he thinks of the card at all, that what stops him from
checking whose hands they were is not indifference but a fairly precise
calculation of what he would do with the answer, whichever answer it
turned out to be, and he has not yet worked out what that would be, and
suspects he does not want to.
