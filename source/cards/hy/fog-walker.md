## Draw

The root of the Fog branch, and the least understood of the three —
concealment that is not hiding, because hiding is a thing a person
chooses and this is not chosen by anyone still recognizably present to do
the choosing. A patrol she is walking is there, and then it is half
there, and nobody hears the difference happen. She does not know how she
does it. She has stopped being asked, because every answer she has ever
given has been *I don't know*, delivered in the same even voice, and a
question that produces the same true answer every time eventually stops
being a question anyone bothers to ask.

## Story

### THE SEVENTH SET OF FOOTPRINTS

The patrol had counted six that morning, correctly, at the gate, the way
every patrol was counted before it went up the mountain road, and had
returned that evening still counting six, correctly, at the same gate, and
it was only the corporal doing the returns — a meticulous man, by
temperament rather than training, the kind who re-adds a column out of
habit rather than doubt — who noticed, three weeks running, that the road
register showed seven sets of prints in the frost past the second
switchback and six everywhere else, and that the seventh set began
without explanation roughly forty paces past where the mist usually
gathered on that stretch and ended, just as abruptly, before it.

He raised it, carefully, with her directly, because raising it with
anyone else first would have felt like reporting a person rather than
asking one, and she told him what she told everyone who asked, which was
that she did not know, in the same register she used for the weather and
the ration count, and he believed her immediately and completely, not
because the answer was reassuring but because nothing in her manner
suggested a person capable of withholding one — she was not being
modest, or careful, or protective of a secret; she genuinely did not
have the information he was asking for, the way a door does not have the
information of who opened it.

What none of them had language for, and what the corporal came closest to
without quite arriving, was that the mist was not a screen she raised.
Concealment implies an intention behind it, a person deciding to be
harder to find, and crediting her with that intention was the mistake
every account of her made, because it let the men around her imagine a
soldier making a choice on their behalf, when what actually happened, six
mornings a week on that road, was that a portion of what made her a
locatable person simply stopped being available to be located, without
her consent, without her knowledge, and without anyone — including her —
able to say in advance which forty paces of road it would happen on next.

The patrol reached the summit outpost that evening at full strength,
seven where the register showed six, and nobody mentioned it at the
debrief, because there was, by the third week, a settled and wordless
agreement among the six men who counted themselves each morning that
whatever walked the gap in the mist with them was doing them a service
no form existed to thank, and that thanking it directly would require
first agreeing on what, exactly, they would be thanking, which none of
them — least of all her — had ever managed to do.
