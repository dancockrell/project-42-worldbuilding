## Draw

Came into the signal hut in August 1941 about the stove, not the meeting,
and was one of four the fusion chose anyway — no rank, no aptitude anyone
had noticed, no belief, no reason to be in the room. He is the reason
every theory the programme has written since has failed, and the eleventh
theory has not addressed him either.

Food he cooks does not spoil. Men who eat at his table sleep without
dreaming, and will not elaborate, and do not need to. He was offered a
commission in 1942. He said no, and the file has never asked him why.

## Story

### THE COMMISSION

The officer who came to offer it was young and correct and had clearly
rehearsed the sentence on the train, because he delivered it without a
single wasted word — the programme wished to recognise Tsuruoka's
contribution with a commission, effective immediately, no examination
required given the circumstances of his selection — and stood afterward in
the kitchen doorway with the particular stillness of a man who has said
something generous and is waiting to be thanked for it, while Tsuruoka
went on turning the rice, which at that hour of the morning required
turning, commission or no commission, and said, after a silence long
enough that the officer began to wonder if he had been understood, that he
would rather keep the stove.

It went into the file exactly that way — *declined; no reason given* — and
the young officer, writing it, had felt a brief, unprofessional urge to add
a second line explaining that the stove had not, in fact, been the
question, and had then thought better of it, on the reasoning that a form
with an unexplained refusal on it was a form nobody would ever ask about
twice, whereas a form that tried to describe the actual conversation would
invite a superior officer to sit down and have it himself, and nothing
about the kitchen had ever survived that kind of attention well.

What the file could not have recorded, because Tsuruoka had not said it
aloud and would not have known how to if pressed, was that a commission
would have moved him out of the kitchen and into a room with the other
three, where a man was expected to have opinions about the fusion and the
treaty and the watching, all subjects Tsuruoka had thought about exactly as
much as he had thought about the stove's third burner before it started
smoking — which was to say, not at all, until it needed fixing — and he had
understood, without working out the reasoning in any order he could have
explained to the young officer, that a stove needing fixing was a problem
with an edge to it, a place where the wanting stopped, and that the other
thing had no edge anywhere he had found, and a man who spends his attention
on a problem with no edge has nothing left over for rice, and rice, unlike
the fusion, would not wait.

He fixed the third burner himself that afternoon, with wire, for the second
time, and it held, and that evening thirty men ate at his table and slept
without dreaming and did not remark on it, having stopped remarking on it
some months before, and Tsuruoka banked the fire and went to sleep himself
without dreaming either, which he had also stopped remarking on, there
being, so far as he had ever been able to determine, nothing useful to say
about a thing that happened to you the same way every night and asked
nothing of you in return except that you not ask it anything back.
