## Draw

A railway clerk in March, this in April — the fusion chose her the way it
chooses everyone, without asking and without explaining, and left her
serene in a way that is wrong for what happens around her, which is the
only tell anyone has ever found. She holds a checkpoint the way she once
held a timetable: exactly, without visible effort, and without once
raising her voice. Nothing gets past a Kitsune-Warden who has decided it
should not, and nobody has ever worked out how she decides.

## Story

### THE TIMETABLE SHE STILL KEEPS

She had kept a timetable for six years before the fusion and she kept one
after it without meaning to, the same private discipline transferred
whole from one life into the next the way a hand keeps its grip on a rail
after the train has already stopped — trucks at 0600, the relief detail
at 1400, the supply run that was late eleven times in April and eleven
times in May, and she noted each lateness the way she had once noted a
delayed express, with no anger in it at all, only the clerk's old
satisfaction of a fact correctly filed.

The men who worked the checkpoint with her had stopped, by the second
month, remarking on how calm she was, because remarking on it had begun
to feel like remarking on the weather, and had started instead to feel,
without examining the feeling closely, a low and constant unease at
shift change that none of them connected to her directly, the way a man
standing near a very quiet machine feels the quiet before he thinks to
ask what the machine usually sounds like.

What none of them had noticed, because noticing it required comparing an
April instant against a March one they had not thought to keep, was that
her stillness now held nothing behind it that resembled patience. Patience
is a woman waiting for something to end. What the checkpoint had instead
was a woman who was not waiting for anything, who watched the road the
way the road itself watched nothing, continuously and without preference,
and who could stand at the barrier from dusk to dawn without once shifting
her weight, not because she was disciplined, but because the part of her
that would have grown tired of standing had gone somewhere the fusion had
not thought to bring back.

A supply truck came through without papers on the ninth night of June,
waved on by a corporal who knew the driver and trusted him for it, and she
stepped into its path without raising her voice or her hand, and the
truck stopped the way trucks stop for a fact rather than for a person —
immediately, and without the driver afterward being entirely sure why he
had done it — and she asked for the papers in the same even register she
asked for everything, and the corporal, watching from the guardhouse,
understood for the first time in nine weeks that he had never once seen
her afraid of anything large enough to be afraid of, and understood,
directly behind that thought and colder than it, that this was not
courage either, and had no name he was willing to go looking for.
