## Draw

Twice-lived, in the folklore's own sense, and the establishment records
this literally rather than as metaphor, because whatever was meant to end
her did not take, and the file is silent on what was attempted. The
fusion came to her faster and more completely than to anyone before or
since, and no explanation was offered for that either, because none was
requested. She is patient in a way the others at the station are not, and
the officers who have studied her file long enough to notice have found
that this is not the same thing as calm. She has outlasted three of them.

## Story

### THE FOURTH OFFICER

The fourth officer assigned to her file was young enough to still believe
that a personnel record, read carefully and in order, would eventually
explain its subject, and had spent his first week doing what the previous
three had also done in their first weeks: reading the two entries that
preceded his own attention, the terse clinical note about an incident the
file called *non-fatal, cause undetermined* and the equally terse note,
filed eleven days later, recording that the fusion had proceeded and had
taken with unusual speed, and had found, as the previous three had also
found, that the two notes sat beside each other on the page without
explaining anything about the distance between them.

He had asked her about it directly in their first interview, in the
careful, roundabout phrasing officers used when they wanted an answer
without appearing to have demanded one, and she had looked at him for a
moment with an expression he would spend a long time afterward failing to
describe accurately in his own notes, and had said only that some things,
once interrupted, do not resume from where they left off, and had offered
nothing further, and he had written *subject declined to elaborate* and
told himself this was a complete account of what had happened in the
room.

What he had not written down, because it had taken him the better part of
a year to notice it clearly enough to name, was that she showed no
impatience with the process — not with the interviews, not with the
delays, not with the slow procedural churn of an establishment that
reassigned her file every eighteen months or so as officers rotated
through and away from her — and that this absence of impatience was not
the absence of urgency it first appeared to be. A patient woman is
waiting for something to arrive. He had come, by his second year on the
file, to suspect she was not waiting at all, that time simply did not
press on her the way it pressed on the men assigned to observe her, each
of whom eventually transferred, or retired, or in one case simply stopped
coming to work and was replaced without explanation, while she remained
in the same chair, across the same desk, exactly as she had been when he
arrived.

He closed the file at the end of his third year on it, the longest any
officer had lasted, and wrote, in the space for a final assessment, a
single honest line that he suspected the next officer would read the way
he had read his three predecessors' — carefully, and without believing it
could possibly mean what it said. *Subject is not the one running out of
time here.*
