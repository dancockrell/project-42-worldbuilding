## Draw

Carries a light that is not fuel and cannot be extinguished on request.
It does not warm anything — the quartermaster measured it twice, expecting
an instrument error the second time, and got the same reading both times.
Filed under equipment, because the alternative filing needs a signature
nobody at the depot is willing to provide. He sleeps beside it and does
not say whether he sleeps. It goes where he goes, and nobody has yet
worked out whether that is his choice or its own.

## Story

### THE REQUISITION LINE THAT DOES NOT EXIST

The quartermaster had solved the problem the way quartermasters solve
every problem the regulations do not anticipate, which was to find the
nearest category the regulations did anticipate and file the unprecedented
thing inside it, and had settled, after two days of turning the matter
over, on *lighting, portable, non-standard*, a line that had not existed
in the depot ledger before that week and existed now because a form
without a line for a thing looks worse, to anyone auditing it later, than
a form with a slightly wrong one, and the light went into the wagon each
morning listed beside the lanterns, weighing nothing, requiring no oil,
requisitioned for nobody's approval because nobody above the quartermaster
had yet asked to see the line closely enough to question it.

He carried it the way a man carries a duty he was never assigned but
cannot now set down, close to his chest through cold marches and closer
still through the ones that were not cold, and had stopped, sometime in
the second month, trying to explain to the men around him what it felt
like to sleep — if sleep was the correct word for what he did — with a
light beside him that gave off no heat and asked nothing of him and simply
continued, hour after hour, doing whatever it was doing, because every
explanation he attempted came out sounding smaller and stranger than the
thing itself, and a man who cannot describe his own condition accurately
eventually stops describing it at all and settles for being asked about
it less.

What nobody at the depot had considered, filing the light under equipment
between the lanterns and the field stoves, was that the filing itself had
answered a question for him that no officer had ever thought to ask
directly — whether the thing beside him belonged to the Empire's
inventory or to him — and had answered it, by the plain fact of the
ledger line, in the Empire's favor, without anyone present intending to
settle anything at all. He had watched it happen, the light being counted,
weighed, and assigned a depot number, and had understood, watching, that
the paperwork's calm acceptance of an impossible object was not comfort,
whatever it looked like from outside; it was the last door quietly
closing on the version of the question that had once included him.

He banked the fire that evening the way he banked it every evening,
though there was nothing in it that needed banking, an old habit performed
now for no reason he could name, and lay down beside a light that did not
warm him, in a tent numbered like every other tent, under a requisition
line that would still be read, years from now, by a clerk who would have
no reason to wonder what it had actually been like to carry.
