## Draw

Four hundred pounds, and it runs down a horse the way a debt runs down a
man, unhurried and certain. The Allies named it. The Office never has —
the file says *Muster 8, variant w*, muzzles being handled stock, named
for the equipment rather than the animal, which is how the men avoid
naming the animal.

It takes commands by whistle at eleven hundred metres and holds a scent
through standing water. Someone removed the howl in 1939 and wrote a
paper about the removal, and the paper is filed, and the howl has not
come back.

## Story

### THE PLATE

By August the ditches along the east dyke had stopped smelling of
anything living and started smelling of the marsh itself, warm and
brown and patient, the way standing water smells when it has given up
trying to go anywhere, and Gefreiter Lindt walked the animal along the
top of that smell every morning with eleven feet of chain in his hand
and nine of it let out, because nine was standing orders, whatever
Pflug said at the gate about wanting more out here.

The Muster went ahead of him with its head low and its shoulders
rolling under a coat gone stiff with old water and soft again with new,
and twice that morning it stopped for a hare and looked back at him
with the patience of something taught, thoroughly, that its own
wanting is not the part that matters, and both times Lindt said nothing
and it went on, the chain never once finding the end of its nine feet.

At the sluice the whistle came from the reeds, two notes, and the
animal's head came up the way a man's head comes up at his own name in
a crowded room. It went into the water without a sound, and it was
already across before Lindt had both hands on the chain, and the chain
ran the rest of itself through his palms and burned there, and he let
it go, because a man who does not let go of eleven feet of chain goes
into the water after it and does not always come out the way the chain
does.

He found it four hundred metres on, past the beet rows, standing over
a boy of perhaps fifteen who had gone down in the wet earth and was not
moving except to breathe, shallow and quick, the way a rabbit breathes
under a hand. The Muster was not touching him. It had set one forefoot
on his coat, pinning cloth and nothing underneath the cloth, ribs going
like a bellows, water sheeting off it into the furrow, looking back
down the field at Lindt, waiting, in no particular hurry, to be told.

The whistle came again from the reeds, unmistakably the same hand. The
animal did not move.

Lindt came up slow through the mud and got the chain and took a turn
of it round his fist, and the Muster let him, and he stood there a
moment with the boy's breathing under his boot and the marsh going on
smelling of itself in every direction, and he decided, without
deciding it in any way he could have written down, that the animal had
not heard the second whistle. Water in the ears. It had only just come
up out of the sluice.

That is what went into the log that evening, under a lamp that smelled
faintly of the same brown water, and Section Six read it on Thursday
with sixty others and agreed. Water in the ears. A maintenance matter.

They brought it in the following week and looked at the ears, out of
what somebody wrote down as thoroughness, and the ears were fine, and
that went into a different file, and nobody has yet been asked to
reconcile the two.
