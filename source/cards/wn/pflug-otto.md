## Draw

Runs the kennels, eleven years, and is kind to what is in them in a way
that has survived everything the Office has asked of it, without once
extending as far as the product, because the product is not animals and
he has always been clear about that.

He keeps a second book, in his own hand, that nobody at Section Six
knows exists — the only accurate record of what came from where in the
whole of the Bestiarium. He has never once used it to contradict a
return, and could not tell you why not.

## Story

### THE OTHER BOOK

The feed returns had not reconciled since 1941, and everyone from the
veterinary officer to Section Six had long since stopped expecting them
to, the discrepancy having become, over the years, a kind of weather
that the establishment simply had, favourable and unremarked and
apparently permanent, the way a house settles and nobody thinks to
mention the crack in the plaster because the house has always had it.

Pflug filled the returns in on the fifteenth of every month, in the
Office's own hand and the Office's own vocabulary, stock and yield and
spoilage, the words doing their work the way they always did, and then,
in the evening, alone, by a lamp that burned kerosene the estimates did
not account for because he bought it himself, he opened the other book
and wrote down what had actually come in, and from where, and under
what name, and he had been doing this for six years without a single
entry ever leaving the room it was written in.

He could not have said, if the veterinary officer had asked him, or if
Section Six had somehow found the will to visit a kennel it administered
entirely by correspondence, what the book was for. It was not evidence,
because he had shown it to no one and did not intend to. It was not
conscience, exactly, because conscience implies a plan for what the
guilt is owed to, and Pflug had no plan; he simply could not make himself
stop writing down what was true once he had started, the way a man who
has always locked a door finds he cannot leave it open even in a house
with nobody in it.

In November a walker had gone down in the frame with Lindt still inside
it, the joint seized in the cold the way they always seized, and Pflug
had gone out with the crew and got him free with his own hands, and
neither of them had ever mentioned it again, there being no form for a
man doing a thing simply because it needed doing, and Pflug had gone
home that night and written, in the other book, under November,
Lindt — out — and nothing else, no reason, because the reason did not
belong to the accounts the book was keeping and he was careful, in that
book most of all, to write only what could be verified.

He kept the ledger in a drawer that locked, in a room that did not, and
he had never asked himself what would happen to it if he did not come
in one morning, and this, more than the discrepancy, more than the
smell of the kennels in August, was the thing that occasionally kept
him from sleeping — not what the book contained, but that it would
simply stop, mid-page, the way an animal stops, and nobody would know
to look for it, or would think, if they found it, to wonder why a man
who filed everything correctly had also, quietly, for six years, filed
the truth.
