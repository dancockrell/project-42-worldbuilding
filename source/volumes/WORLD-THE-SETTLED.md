# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME VII: THE SETTLED

The people Camp Iron Bell pulled out of time. Characters the games can use
directly.

All invented. No real historical figure appears on this roster, and the reason is
structural rather than squeamish — see §1.

---

## 1. The constraint that makes this roster interesting

**A retrieval only closes if the subject's absence is already accommodated by the
surviving record** (the Consistency Finding). You cannot lift a person history is
certain about.

So the programme set out to mine greatness and discovered it could only reach
**the people history was careless with.**

Not the famous. The overlooked, the vanished, the misfiled, the presumed
drowned, the published-under-someone-else's-name, the ones whose papers burned.
Every person on this roster is someone the twentieth century dropped, and Camp
Iron Bell caught them on the way down.

**That is the faction's whole tragedy in a filing rule.** The Tesla denial in
Cabinet Nine is what happens when they reach for someone history noticed.

It is also, unavoidably, a roster weighted toward people the record neglected
*because of who they were* — and the programme has never written that observation
down, because writing it down would require someone to have made it.

### How many

**Nineteen, ever.** Retrieval is ruinous: a night's work, three towns browned
out, and a cost line Section VI would recognise. This is not an army. It is a
small strange household of people who cannot go home, in married quarters nobody
is married in.

Keep the number small in every medium. The programme's power is not scale.

## 2. The roster

### Corporal Adaeze Bright — Current

**From 2031.** Retrieved July 1943, the second successful closure.

Reads a live wire the way other people read weather. Holds current across a
chain of people without killing them, which nobody at Iron Bell can explain and
which she has stopped offering to explain.

*Why the record lost her:* it did not, in her own century — it lost her in ours.
She has no birth certificate here, no service number that predates her issue, and
W.D. 42-B has a box for that which nobody has ever been able to fill in
satisfactorily.

*Her contradiction:* the programme's regulations do not care where anyone is
from. Copiah County does. She is billeted separately by state ordinance, has
twice requested to be quartered with the four men she is wired to, and has been
denied on grounds of local law. She keeps every denial. Says it is for the
memoir.

*Voice:* dry, fast, entirely unimpressed by 1944. Does not perform gratitude.

---

### Nkemdirim Osei — Chrono, the programme's best mathematician

**From 1911.** Retrieved November 1943.

Solved the closure problem that made Frame Three work, six weeks after arriving,
having been shown the notation once.

*Why the record lost him:* he published two papers in 1909 under his supervisor's
name, as arranged, and died in the 1911 cholera outbreak at twenty-six with
nothing in print bearing his own. History is not certain about him. History has
never heard of him.

*His contradiction:* he has read the papers. Both of them. In the camp library,
in his supervisor's name, in a bound volume with a card in the back. He said
nothing about it and requested a transfer to the Line Shed, which was denied
because he is too useful where he is.

*Voice:* courteous, precise, and increasingly silent. The most dangerous person
at Iron Bell to underestimate and the one most likely to be underestimated.

---

### Able Seaman Tomás Reyes-Whitlock — Phase

**From 1907.** Retrieved June 1943. The first successful closure.

The most attenuated person alive. Has phased more than anyone and is, by Falk's
own unsigned estimate, somewhere past the point where the bands mean anything.

*Why the record lost him:* went overboard from a merchant ship off the Grand
Banks in fog, presumed drowned, no body. The cleanest 42-D anyone has ever filed.

*His contradiction:* he is the programme's proof of concept and its warning, and
it cannot stand to be either. **People at Iron Bell have started not noticing him
in rooms.** Not avoiding — *not noticing*, and then apologising when he speaks.
He has begun sitting nearer to doors.

*Voice:* patient, gentle, a little slow, as though speaking through water. Does
not complain. Has asked Falk one question, twice, and she has not answered it.

---

### Dr Miriam Halloran-Sze — the programme's physician before Falk

**From 1889.** Retrieved August 1943. **Deceased, February 1944.**

*Why the record lost her:* a workhouse infirmary doctor in Whitechapel whose
entire caseload was people nobody counted. She vanished from the record in 1889
and no one looked.

*What happened:* she died of a stroke, at Iron Bell, at fifty-four, in a bed. The
first settled death and the one nobody had a form for. She is buried in the
county under a name the programme invented, because burying her under her own
would have required explaining a woman born in 1835.

*Her use to the games:* she is the reason Naomi Falk stopped believing the
coherence bands. Halloran-Sze read the 42-C tables in her first week and said
they were written to be signed, not to be true. Falk has her notes and has never
filed them.

### The other fifteen

Named, sketched, and available. Use them, extend them, do not contradict them.

- **Ilse Bregenz**, 1922, chemist. Papers burned in a laboratory fire that also
  removed her from the record. Runs the Seuche countermeasure work and will not
  discuss what she thinks Hollernbruch is doing, because she is from close enough
  to know.
- **Sgt Wendell Pike**, 1863, artilleryman. Presumed lost at Chickamauga. Cannot
  read. Has an unmatched instinct for where a shell will land and has been asked
  to explain it forty times.
- **Yeva Sorokina**, 1919, cryptographer. Lost in the Russian civil war, one of
  several million the record simply does not account for. Trusts nobody at Iron
  Bell and is correct not to.
- **The Dutchman**, c. 1650, name unrecorded even by the programme. The earliest
  retrieval and the least successful. Speaks to nobody, works in the kitchen
  garden, and is the only person at Iron Bell who is visibly, permanently
  frightened. **Nobody has ever established whether he understands where he is.**
- **Fen Cheung**, 2004, structural engineer. Forward-settled. Asked what the two
  fountains outside Building 1 were for on her first morning. See Volume III §6.
- **Ensign Tobias Ferrer**, 1799, naval surgeon. Taken mid-voyage; the muster
  book carries him as lost overboard off Cape Finisterre, a common enough entry
  in 1799 that nobody at the Admiralty ever queried it. *His contradiction:* he
  did not drown and did not volunteer, and he has never once let the programme
  forget the difference between those two facts. Used by The Long Night as a
  playable Chrono character — see `longnight-godot`.
- **Marta Kell**, 2011, analyst. **Forward-settled**, like Cheung. Retrieved
  knowing full well what several of the next eighty years contain, and telling
  nobody, per the unwritten rule above — enforced on herself before it was ever
  enforced on her. *Her contradiction:* everyone at Iron Bell has stopped
  trusting her exactly because she is always right, and being right is the only
  thing she has left of home. Used by The Long Night as a playable Chrono
  character.
- **Nadia Ruhl**, 1926, Phase. Survived an event the programme's own criteria
  file lists dryly as "a qualifying loss with multiple qualifying candidates" —
  she was the one taken. She has read that file. *Her contradiction:* she does
  not resent being chosen. She resents that a form exists which had to choose
  between her and someone else, and that whoever wrote it has never had to meet
  the someone else. Used by The Long Night as a playable Phase character.
- Seven more, deliberately unwritten, so the games have room. **Add them here
  when you make them**, do not keep them in a game's local file.

## 3. How to write the settled

- **They are tired, displaced and frequently bored.** Not oracles, not sages, not
  quipping time-travellers. The war is depressingly familiar and the plumbing is
  miraculous.
- **Nobody asks a forward-settled how it comes out.** Unwritten, absolutely
  enforced. Two men who did are *reassigned* in the files.
- **The Thursday card game** in the Annex has accreted rules from four centuries
  and is unplayable by anyone who was not present when a rule was added. They will
  teach it to you. You will not understand it. That is the point.
- **The word "settled" was chosen in Washington by someone who has never been
  here.** They use it too, with an inflection.
- **They do not thank the programme.** Not one of them, ever, in any file. Nobody
  at Iron Bell has remarked on this.
