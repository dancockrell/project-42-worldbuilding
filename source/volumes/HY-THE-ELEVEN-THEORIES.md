# HYAKKI YAKŌ — THE ELEVEN THEORIES

**Fork C.** Every attempt the programme has made to explain the selection, in
order, with the reason each failed.

All eleven are in the cabinet. None has been withdrawn. **The programme has never
formally abandoned a theory** — it simply files the next one on top.

Sakaguchi Tomo is compiling the twelfth and already knows it will fail.

---

## I — Proximity (Sept 1941)
*It selects whoever is nearest.*

Failed within a week. Two of the four at Light Seven were across the room from
each other; the petty officer was closest to the window and was not taken.

## II — Aptitude (Oct 1941)
*It selects the capable.*

The most popular theory and the shortest-lived. **The cook.**

## III — Belief (Nov 1941)
*It selects the devout.*

Written by a chaplain and filed with enthusiasm. Of the first four, one was
observant, one was not, one had converted twice, and Tsuruoka, asked, said he had
never given it thought and did not intend to start.

## IV — Lineage (Dec 1941)
*It selects along family lines.*

The programme spent four months and considerable money tracing ancestry. **Found
nothing, and the report is nine hundred pages.** It is the longest document in the
cabinet and it says no.

*This theory is the faction's one genuinely ugly episode and the volume does not
soften it: the men who commissioned it wanted a particular answer, and did not get
it, and filed the result anyway. That they filed it honestly is the only credit
available and it is real.*

## V — Circumstance (Feb 1942)
*It selects at moments of crisis.*

Fits eleven of the first fourteen. Fails on Iwasaki Chiyo, who was mending a
sleeve.

## VI — Consent (Apr 1942)
*It selects those willing.*

Attractive, dignified, and dead on arrival: roughly two hundred volunteers have
never been selected, and several of the selected were not asked and would have
said no.

## VII — Sound (May 1942)
*Something audible precedes it.*

The only theory built from a first-hand account rather than from a preference —
Ueda Sanae's carrier tone. **Tested more rigorously than any other theory and
found nothing.** Its author was reassigned and the theory was not.

*Sakaguchi has re-opened this one privately. She has not said so.*

## VIII — Place (Jul 1942)
*Certain sites are eligible.*

Died on Kanazawa. A shop. No signal apparatus, no water, no history, nothing.

## IX — Number (Sept 1942)
*The selections follow a series.*

Four, then two, then five, then one. Three officers worked on this for six months
and produced a sequence that fits perfectly up to the point at which it stops
fitting, which is the eighteenth selection.

**The paper is still cited internally.** It is the most mathematically competent
document the programme has produced and it is about nothing.

## X — Utility (Nov 1942)
*It selects what it needs.*

Requires knowing what it wants. **Article I of the treaty asserts the First Party
makes no claim to know that.** The theory was filed anyway, which meant the
programme's own foundational document contradicted its working hypothesis for
four months and nobody raised it.

## XI — Attention (Mar 1943)
*It selects those already observed.*

The most sophisticated one, and the closest to the physics: if being observed is
what makes a fact, perhaps it takes what it is already looking at.

**Circular, and its author knew.** He wrote the objection into his own conclusion,
which is more than any of the other ten did, and then filed it because the
deadline was the deadline.

---

## What all eleven have in common

**Ten of them were written to be true rather than to be tested.** They begin with
a shape somebody wanted — merit, faith, blood, willingness — and go looking.

Theory VII is the exception and it is the one that got tested hardest and
disproved fastest, which is a lesson about the programme that the programme has
not drawn.

**And every one had to account for the cook.** He is in all eleven. He defeats all
eleven. He came in about the stove.

---

## The twelfth

Sakaguchi's, in progress, and she has told nobody what it proposes.

What is known: she has requested Ueda's original account, the Kanazawa visit
records for all four years, and — unusually — **the file on the two hundred who
were never selected.**

Nobody has previously thought the unchosen were data.

> *Not in the file: what she thinks the unchosen are evidence of.*
