# HYAKKI YAKŌ — THE UNSENT LETTERS

**Fork C.** The faction's primary document format (Volume IX), collected.

Every fused person at Light Seven writes. **Almost none of it is sent.** Not
censorship — the programme's censor has almost nothing to do and has said so in a
return. They write and they keep them.

Kurosawa has a drawer. Nobody has counted it.

---

## Why this is the faction's format

Werk Nachtigall speaks in requisitions because it is a department. Project 42
speaks in forms because it is a bureaucracy that means well.

**Hyakki Yakō speaks in letters that were never sent, because it is full of
people who cannot say this to anyone who could hear it**, and who keep writing
anyway.

The register that makes it work: **they are not writing about the horror.** They
are writing about the weather, and a debt, and whether the roof got fixed, and
the horror is what the letter is standing next to.

## The letters

### Kurosawa Ren, undated, to his sister

> The food here is better than you would think, because of the cook, who is one
> of us and does not act like it.
>
> I have not answered your question about coming home at the New Year and I am
> not going to answer it in this letter either. Ask me again in the spring and I
> will have something to tell you.
>
> The drum is fine. You would say it is too loud. It is exactly loud enough.

*Nine such letters. All to the same sister. None sent.*

---

### Ueda Sanae, March 1944, to her old instructor at the signal school

> You taught us that if you hear something on a frequency the set cannot receive,
> the fault is in the set.
>
> I have had the set apart four times.
>
> I am well. The work is interesting. I would like to come and see you when there
> is leave, and I would like to bring a question, and it is not a question about
> equipment, and I think you may be the only person who would not report me for
> asking it.

*Not sent. Filed by nobody. It is in her locker.*

---

### Andō Michi, no date, to her brother, who is forty metres away

> You were in the boat.
>
> I have started six letters and they all begin like that, which is why none of
> them are finished. I am going to leave this one at that and see whether I can
> hand it to you.

*She has not.*

---

### Iwasaki Chiyo, Kanazawa, to the officer who visits

> Lieutenant —
>
> You asked whether anything unusual had occurred and I said no. I have said no
> eleven times now, which you have written down eleven times, and I would like
> you to know that I have counted and I expect you have not.
>
> You are very polite. You always take the tea. I notice you never ask the
> questions in a different order, which means you are reading them, which means
> somebody wrote them for you.
>
> **I would like to meet whoever wrote them.**
>
> I am not going to give you this. Next time you come I will say no, and you will
> write no, and we will both do our jobs. But I wanted it written down somewhere
> that I knew.

*In a box under the counter of a shop in Kanazawa. The programme has never
searched the premises. It has never occurred to anyone that she might keep
records too.*

---

### Sgt Mifune Kaoru, 1943, to nobody, one line

> I asked for the storm.

*Found by an orderly, returned to his locker unread beyond the first line,
because the orderly stopped.*

---

### Unsigned, undated, in the shrine offering box rather than a locker

> I do not know if this is how one does it.
>
> I am not asking for anything. I know you do not answer and I am not writing to
> be answered. I wanted to say that the roof over the fog signal was repaired in
> October and it took four men two days, and that Ogawa turned sixty-eight in
> November, and that there is a heron that comes to the rocks in the evening and
> has done every evening since I came here.
>
> I do not know whether you see these things or whether you only see us.
>
> That is all. It was a good year for the light.

**Nobody knows who wrote this.** Hoshino found it, read it, and put it back.

*It is still in the box.*

---

## Writing more of these

- **The horror is never the subject.** It is the roof, the heron, the New Year,
  the tea. The horror is the room the letter is written in.
- **Nobody complains.** Not one of them. That absence is the loudest thing here.
- **They are addressed to somebody specific** — a sister, an instructor, a
  brother, a lieutenant. Even the one in the offering box has a reader in mind.
- **Never write one from the watcher.** It does not write. It does not answer.
- **Keep them short.** The best one here is five words long.
