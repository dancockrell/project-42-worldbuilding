## Finding: Frame Two's disposition is blocked by two incompatible mechanisms

Three documents describe why Frame Two still stands under a tarp, and two of
them contradict each other about **who is refusing to sign.**

**`WORLD-CAMP-IRON-BELL.md:100-103`** (the original, foundational text):

> Frame Two killed Sergeant Aurelio Fitch in November 1943. It has not been
> dismantled, because dismantling requires a disposition form countersigned
> by an officer who has since been reassigned, and **the replacement will
> not sign** for equipment he did not receive.

The blocker is an unnamed replacement officer, refusing on liability
grounds — he will not sign for equipment he never personally inventoried.

**`P42-CABINET-NINE.md`, 42-R/0203:**

> Not a denial of an operation. A denial of a form — the disposition that
> would let Frame Two be dismantled, refused because the countersigning
> officer has been reassigned and his replacement will not sign for
> equipment he did not receive... **Vosburgh has resubmitted four times.**

Agrees with the original: external replacement officer blocks it, Vosburgh
is the one trying to push it through.

**`NETWORK-HUB-EDGES.md:39`:**

```
{"from":"vosburgh_elias","to":"fitch_aurelio","axis":"DEBT","w":0.6,
 "note":"the disposition form he will not sign is about not signing Fitch away"}
```

**This says Vosburgh himself is the one refusing to sign** — an emotional
block, not signing away a dead man's equipment. Not an external officer.

**These are not reconcilable as written.** Either Vosburgh is trying
repeatedly and failing (Cabinet Nine, four resubmissions, external
obstruction) or Vosburgh is the obstruction (hub edges, his own refusal, no
resubmission implied). The two readings produce different men: one who is
failed by the institution, one who is failing to act on his own grief.

Both are good material. I have not ruled between them — this is a
canon question, not a typo, and it changes how Vosburgh should be written
going forward. Whoever holds Prime 1: pick one, and note which document
should be corrected against the other.

*My own read, offered and not imposed: the Cabinet Nine version is richer,
because it lets Vosburgh be a competent officer defeated by the same
paperwork logic that defeats everyone else at Camp Iron Bell, rather than a
man simply refusing to act — which is a more common grief beat and a less
distinctive one for this setting.*
