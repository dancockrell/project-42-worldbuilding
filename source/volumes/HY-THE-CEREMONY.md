# HYAKKI YAKŌ — THE CEREMONY

**Fork C.** The rite, written out. Companion to the treaty: the programme's other
central artefact, and the other thing it does not know is working.

---

## 1. Status

**Nobody knows whether any of it matters.**

Fusions have occurred without it — the four at Light Seven in August 1941 had no
ceremony, because there was no programme yet. Fusions have failed to occur with
it, correctly performed, on candidates who wanted it badly.

**The programme performs it in full, every time, and the reason is not belief.**
Stopping would be an admission that the Empire has no part in this: that it did
not achieve a contact, negotiate terms, or select its own soldiers, but was
simply the room something walked into.

So the ceremony is performed correctly. This appears not to have mattered. The
next one is scheduled.

---

## 2. The rite

Ninety minutes. Prescribed. Written down in a single bound volume kept in the
same cabinet as the treaty.

**I — The Reading of the Instrument.**
Articles I, III and VI are read aloud. Not the whole treaty. **Never Article IX.**
The abbreviation was made in 1942 for time and has never been revisited.

**II — The Statement of Origin.**
The candidate states their name, birthplace, and the name of one person who would
know them. The third item was Amatsu's addition and she has not explained it.

**III — The Interval.**
Twenty minutes. Nothing happens. The candidate sits. The officiant sits.
**This is the longest part and the only part nobody has ever proposed shortening.**

**IV — The Offering.**
Rice, water, and one object belonging to the candidate, which is not returned.
The objects are kept. There is a shelf.

**V — The Naming.**
The officiant names the folklore to be fused. **This is the part that most
obviously does nothing** — the programme has never once been correct in advance
about what a subject would receive, and the naming is a guess made from the
candidate's occupation, which Theory II should have discredited.

**VI — The Close.**
The officiant states that the First Party has performed its obligations. The
candidate is asked whether they wish to proceed.

**Everyone says yes.** In three years, nobody has said no, and the programme has
never asked why the question is still being put.

---

## 3. What happens next

Usually nothing.

**Of one hundred and nine ceremonies performed since 1941, thirty-seven were
followed by a fusion.** The interval between rite and fusion has ranged from
immediate to eleven months. Sakaguchi's records are the only reason those numbers
exist.

The seventy-two who were not selected went back to their units, or stayed as
unchosen support, and **attended the next ceremony.**

---

## 4. The shelf

Item V, the objects. They are kept because the rite says kept and nobody has
authorised disposal.

A comb. A tobacco tin. A photograph face-down, which was face-down when it was
placed and has not been turned over. Two hundred and eleven items on four
shelves, labelled by date and candidate.

**Seventy-two of them belong to people who are still alive and still at the
station**, and could ask for them back, and have not.

Petty Officer Hoshino dusts this too.

---

## 5. Writing it

- **Play it completely straight.** No irony in the performance. The officiant
  believes in the procedure even if not in the outcome, and the difference
  matters.
- **The Interval is the good part.** Twenty minutes of two people sitting in a
  room is the most Hyakki Yakō thing in the project — nothing is happening,
  something might be, nobody can tell.
- **Never confirm or deny efficacy.** §00. The Shrine branch may be entirely
  decorative and the player is never told.
- **Article IX is never read.** If a scene includes the reading, it stops at VI.
  Do not have anyone remark on the omission.

---

## 6. Open, deliberately

- Why Amatsu added the third item to the Statement of Origin.
- Whether anyone has ever wanted to say no at VI.
- Whose photograph.
