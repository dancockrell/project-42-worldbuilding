# WERK NACHTIGALL — DOCTRINE

**Fork B.** How the Office's product is actually used, and what it is like to
fight.

*No volume number, per the numbering ruling. Filenames carry identity.*

This file exists because of a measured constitutional violation: the shipped card
pool has Werk Nachtigall at **39% win rate, weakest faction by 25 points**, which
contradicts rule 5 (*monstrous and capable*) in the medium. **Fiction cannot
out-argue a win rate.** This is the lore side of the answer — what their strength
actually is, so mechanics has somewhere to put it.

---

## 1. They are not an army. They are a supplier.

**The first thing to understand and the thing most often got wrong.**

Werk Nachtigall does not command troops. It is a **Reichsamt** — an office. It
manufactures, it allocates, and it **issues to formations that are not its own.**

So a Werk Nachtigall unit in the field is on somebody else's establishment, under
somebody else's orders, with a Hollernbruch return still owed on it. The
infantry major who receives eight [walkers](docs/wiki/things/the-bestiary.md) did
not ask for them, cannot refuse them, and must account for them.

**Nobody in the army wants this material.** It is unreliable, it is expensive
against their appropriation, it frightens their own men, and refusing it is not
available.

That is the faction's field-level comedy and it is real: **the horror arrives
with paperwork attached, and the officer receiving it is annoyed.**

---

## 2. The doctrine: attrition is the plan, not the failure

**Nothing they do is elegant.** No teleporting, no phasing, no rewinding — those
belong to the other two programmes and are the thing Werk Nachtigall could not
get.

So the doctrine was built around what they *do* have, which is **material, and
the willingness to spend it.**

### The three principles, as the Office would state them

**1. Presence is cheaper than manoeuvre.** A thing that is simply *there*, and
continues to be there, costs less than a thing that must be moved cleverly. The
Office does not attempt surprise. It attempts **arrival**.

**2. Attrition is an input.** Adopted as doctrine 1942 and it is not a figure of
speech — the kennel line *improves* as the kennel is consumed. Loss is not
failure; loss is the mechanism. A commander who reports heavy losses and holds
the ground has performed to projection.

**3. The establishment is the weapon.** Not the unit. Individual product is
mediocre and the Office knows it. **What is difficult is removing all of it.**

### What that means at the sharp end

**Werk Nachtigall does not win engagements. It refuses to lose ground.**

They are slow, they telegraph, they can be outrun by a walking man, and none of
that is the point. The point is that in six hours they are still there, and there
are more of them, and the position has not moved.

**Mechanical direction, since rule 5 requires the pool to reflect this:** their
power belongs in **persistence, scaling, attrition and denial** — hardest faction
to remove from a sector, strongest late, rewarded for losses. **Not tempo, not
card advantage, not acceleration.** Those are Project 42's and the asymmetry is
canon. A Werk Nachtigall deck that wins by going fast is a design error, not a
balance decision.

## 3. What it is like to fight them

**Not a duel. A schedule.**

- **You will see it coming.** Everything telegraphs. This is not a mercy.
- **It does not stop.** There is no morale, no rout, no moment where it decides
  the position is not worth it. **A [Muster 12](docs/wiki/things/the-bestiary.md)
  has no opinion about whether it is winning.**
- **Ground becomes unavailable rather than contested.** Seuche does not charge; it
  occupies. There is no moment at which the room changed.
- **The whistle arrives before the thing does**, and there is a man behind it who
  you will never see.

### The thing that actually frightens soldiers

Not the product. **The paperwork.**

Captured Werk Nachtigall documents are the most disturbing material the Allies
recover, and intelligence officers have noted that men who have fought the
product are more disturbed by the requisitions than by the fight — because the
requisitions are **routine**, and the routine implies a scale the fight does not.

*Four hundred muzzles, approved in part.* You can work out what two hundred more
means. Nobody has to tell you.

## 4. How the army works around them

Real doctrine develops around unwanted equipment, and this is where the faction
is funniest and most human.

- **The receiving officer's problem is the return**, not the enemy. Material lost
  in action must be accounted for. Material lost *carelessly* is a query from
  Section VI.
- So units develop **elaborate conventions for losing things correctly.** There
  is a right way to write off a walker and a wrong way, and every experienced
  quartermaster in the eastern establishment knows the difference.
- **The two men still in the field** — recovery costed twice, declined twice,
  entered as *present* — are known about. Formations pass the grid reference
  along informally, the way you would mention a bad crossing.
- Nobody has proposed a rescue. **There is no form for it**, and the men who
  would have to file it are the men who would have to explain why.

---

## 5. Interaction with the other two programmes

- **Against Project 42.** The Office knows an Allied apparatus does something to
  distance and cannot reproduce it. Its counter-doctrine is unglamorous and
  effective: **you cannot phase through a position that is entirely occupied.**
  Attenuation is a problem of degree, and Werk Nachtigall's answer to degree is
  volume.
- **Against Hyakki Yakō.** No doctrine exists, because they have met them
  perhaps twice. What exists instead is **one file, eleven weeks long, and a
  conclusion of one sentence.** The Office does not have a plan for the fused. It
  has an unsatisfied research interest, and that is worse.
