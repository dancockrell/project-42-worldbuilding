# PROJECT 42 — CABINET NINE

**Fork A.** The denials. Building 1, bottom drawer.

**"Filed in nine"** is camp slang for anything that will not happen. There are
more W.D. 42-Rs in that drawer than of every other form combined.

A denial is the most characteristic document Camp Iron Bell produces, because it
is the only one that says what the programme *cannot* do — and the programme's
whole voice is built on never saying that anywhere else.

---

## 1. The form

> **W.D. FORM 42-R — NOTICE OF DENIAL**
>
> Reference: _______   Originating section: _______
> Ground of denial (tick one): ☐ cost ☐ consistency ☐ condition ☐ other
> Resubmission permitted: ☐ yes ☐ no
> Authorising officer: _______

**Four grounds and no space to explain any of them.** The *other* box is used
more than the other three together, and there is nowhere on the form to say what
other means. That is not an oversight; the form was printed in 1943 by a
department that did not expect the programme to be denying things it could not
name.

---

## 2. The drawer

Selected, and each of these is quotable.

### 42-R/0007 — the Tesla denial

> Ground: **consistency.** Resubmission permitted: **no.**

The one people know about. Camp Iron Bell runs on his seized papers, does not
understand them, and cannot go and get him: he died on 7 January 1943 in the
Hotel New Yorker, the body was found, the death certified, the papers seized.
**History is certain about Nikola Tesla.**

Somebody filed it anyway. **The interesting thing is not the denial, it is that
the application exists** — somebody at this camp sat down, filled in a 42-A for
Nikola Tesla, and submitted it in the genuine expectation of an answer.

The application is stapled behind the denial. It is in Deel's handwriting.

*New personnel are occasionally shown this. It is a small ceremony nobody would
admit to performing.*

### 42-R/0114 — Bright, billeting

> Ground: **other.** Resubmission permitted: **yes.**

Request to be quartered with the four men she is wired to. Denied on grounds of
local ordinance, which does not fit any of the four boxes, so *other* was ticked
and nothing was written.

**Resubmission permitted: yes.** She resubmitted. It was denied again, on the
same ground, and the second denial is also marked resubmission permitted.

She keeps both. She says they are for the memoir.

### 42-R/0088 — Osei, transfer to Line Shed

> Ground: **other.** Resubmission permitted: **no.**

The *no* is the unusual part. Almost nothing in Cabinet Nine is marked
non-resubmittable, and this is a routine internal transfer request from a man who
solved the closure problem that made Frame Three work.

The reason is not on the form. **The reason is that he is too useful where he
is**, and Wexford's regard for him is the mechanism of his confinement.

Osei has not resubmitted anything since.

### 42-R/0203 — Frame Two, disposition

> Ground: **other.** Resubmission permitted: **yes.**

Not a denial of an operation. A denial of a *form* — the disposition that would
let Frame Two be dismantled, refused because the countersigning officer has been
reassigned and his replacement will not sign for equipment he did not receive.

**So the machine that killed Sergeant Fitch stands under a tarp because a
signature block has nobody in it.** Vosburgh has resubmitted four times.

### 42-R/0009 — milk cooler, claim for damages, $11.00

> Ground: **cost.** Resubmission permitted: **no.**

A dairy farmer three miles east lost eleven dollars of milk to the brown-outs and
wrote four letters about it. Two are in the file.

**The claim was denied on cost.** Eleven dollars, denied on cost, by an
installation that spends what four medium tanks cost on a single night's work.

Nobody has ever put those two facts on the same page. **This document does it by
existing.**

### 42-R/0141 — Falk, request for a second medical officer

> Ground: **cost.** Resubmission permitted: **yes.**

She has not resubmitted. See her file: there is no other doctor, and a physician
who refuses to sign is replaced by one who will, and she has done the arithmetic
on who that would be.

---

## 3. What the drawer is for

The programme's official voice never admits limitation. Cabinet Nine is where the
limitations are, sorted by date, in a form with four boxes and no room to explain
any of them.

**Read the drawer and you have read the programme honestly**, which is why it is
in a bottom drawer and why the slang for hopelessness is its number.

---

## 4. Writing more

- **A denial is funnier and worse than a refusal spoken aloud.** Use the form.
- **The *other* box is the one to reach for.** Anything genuinely interesting does
  not fit the three named grounds.
- ***Resubmission permitted: yes* is often the cruel part**, not the denial.
- **Never explain a denial on the form.** There is nowhere to.
