# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME VIII: THE BESTIARY

What Werk Nachtigall makes, what it is called, and what it is like to meet.

For World Aflame's card pool and Ghost Front's opposition. **Naming decisions
here are made, not proposed.** Use these names; if one is wrong, say so and it
changes for everybody at once.

---

## 1. The two-name rule

**Every creature has two names, and neither is a monster name.**

**The designation** is what the Office calls it: a *Muster* number inside a
procedure, in the estimates, on a form. **The Office does not name monsters. It
numbers products.** Nothing Hollernbruch produces has ever been given a
frightening name by the people who made it, because to them it is not
frightening, it is *late*, or *over budget*, or *performing to projection*.

**The troop name** is what Allied soldiers call it. Short, ugly, English, and
arrived at by men who needed a word fast. Soldiers do not name things
poetically. They name them for a smell, a sound, or a joke.

The gap between the two names is the faction in miniature, and it is free
characterisation on every card.

### The rule that makes it sing

**Where possible, the troop name should be accidentally true.** A joke a soldier
makes without knowing the paperwork agrees with him is §00 in a single word —
the question handed over, unanswered, in something the player finds funny before
they find it horrible.

*Patients* below is the worked example and it is the best one. Use it as the
standard.

## 2. Kadaver — the surgical wing

### Muster 12 · "patients"

**Allied troops call them patients because they move slowly**, the way a man
moves down a hospital corridor. It is a joke about how easy they are to see
coming.

**The Office also calls them patients.** They came out of a surgical theatre;
that is simply what the form says. Two entirely different meanings sitting on the
same word, and neither side knows the other uses it.

Armoured, reinforced, deliberate. Telegraphs everything — it must, because the
frames it wears cannot be hurried and a fast movement would tear the mountings
out of a chest that has been opened and closed nine times.

**Meeting one is not a shock. It is a negotiation with a schedule.** You can see
what it is going to do. You have time. The horror is that having time does not
help as much as it should, and that it is *patient* in the other sense, and it
was made by someone with a medical degree who went home at six.

### Muster 12/b · "seconds"

A Muster 12 rebuilt after failure. The Office's line item reads *reissue*.

The men call them seconds the way you say seconds of a meal, and also the way you
say a second-quality item in a shop, and they mean both.

## 3. Bestiarium — kennels and frames

### Baureihe 7 · "whistlers"

Fast, low, and it closes distance. The one you cannot outrun and the reason
anyone needs to phase.

**Called whistlers because they answer a whistle.** Allied troops worked this out
in the field before intelligence did: the things do not hunt independently, they
respond, and somewhere behind them a man is blowing.

That is worse than an animal and every soldier who has faced one knows it. **The
horror is not the thing coming at you. It is that it was sent.**

*Note for writers: never show the handler. The whistle is enough, and a whistle
with nobody visible behind it is the whole faction.*

### Muster 3 (kennels) · "the litter"

Pesthund stock — the Seuche-fed kennel line from the Volume IV correspondence.
Improves as the kennel is consumed, because the kennel is fed itself. The cost
variance is favourable and Accounts has noted it approvingly.

Troops do not have a good name for these and it shows. *The litter* is what stuck
and it is said flatly, without a joke in it, which is unusual and is the point.

### Gestell 4 · "walkers" — and they are a joke

The exoframes. **They do not work well.** Heavy, slow, they fail in cold, and a
man in one cannot get out unassisted.

Allied troops call them walkers with heavy sarcasm, because they barely do.
**Werk Nachtigall keeps funding them** because a decade of estimates purchased
this, and admitting it walks worse than a man would require somebody to write
that down.

**This one is genuinely funny and should be played that way** (§0). A walker
stuck in mud, with a man inside it who cannot get out, while the war happens
elsewhere, is the exact register — and the man cannot get out.

## 4. Seuche — the cultures

### Verfahren Seuche, Muster 4 · "bakers"

**Named for the smell**, which is warm and yeasty and entirely wrong, and which
Hollernbruch's own staff describe to each other in exactly those terms and to
outsiders not at all (Volume IV §1).

**Reversed 28 Aug 2026: this is Seuche's live end, not its only end.**
Muster 4 is a culture that takes a host and drives it while the host is
still alive, in the sense that matters to a biologist and in no sense that
matters to anyone else. Elsewhere in Seuche's own directorate, the dead
are raised outright (Volume IV §3) — and the paperwork does not
distinguish the two on its face, because both are filed under the same
agricultural vocabulary. A requisition marked *risen* no longer tells you
which procedure produced it.

**A baker is not a duel. It is a room becoming unavailable.** They do not charge.
They occupy. Ground you held ten minutes ago is now ground you cannot cross, and
there was no moment at which that changed.

*The correct fear is spatial and slow.* Ghost Front has this exactly right.

### Verfahren Seuche, Muster 6 · "the quiet ones"

Late-stage. Fully driven, no reflex left that belongs to the host.

They do not smell of bread. **That is how you know it is a six and not a four**,
and the men who worked that out did so the hard way, and it is in no manual.

## 5. What Hyakki Yakō is not

**The fused are not monsters and there is no Hyakki Yakō bestiary. This is a
decision, not an omission.**

Every creature in this volume is a *product* — something the Office made, out of
material, on a budget. Hyakki Yakō's people are **people**, taken by something
that did not ask, doing terrible things without owning the hands doing them
(Volume V, and the guard in LORE-BIBLE.md §5).

The moment they get a bestiary entry they become creatures, and the caricature
that Allied propaganda manufactured industrially in this exact period is one
short step away. **Do not take it.**

Allied troops in 1944 would absolutely have coined ugly names for them. **We do
not use those names**, and the reason is in §1a of the art spec: we never depict
one nation through another's eyes. If a P42 character uses such a word in
dialogue, the game does not endorse it and the player should feel the gap.

Project 42 has no monsters either. Attenuation is a condition, not a creature.

## 6. Writing rules

- **Rule 4 reversed 28 Aug 2026: the occult is available here, and it is not
  an excuse.** Every creature is still scalpels, cultures, machinery, budget
  — and now also the Prussian confidence that a summoning circle is the same
  discipline as a surgical theatre. Necromancy, hybrids grown as much from
  ritual as from genetics, and the raised dead are in. What has not changed:
  real atrocities (Fischer, Mengele, Ishii, the camps, die Ostordnung) stay
  material and documented, and nothing supernatural ever explains any of
  them. The occult is this faction's register, never a cause for anything
  real.
- **Nothing they do is elegant.** They cover ground by running at you, reach you
  with mass, and solve problems by applying more of themselves. No teleporting,
  no phasing, no rewinding — those belong to the other two factions and are the
  thing Werk Nachtigall cannot have.
- **The Office's voice never describes them as frightening.** *Performing to
  projection. Held pending clarification. Attrition against projection.*
- **Menace is supplied by the player, never the speaker.**
- **Show the paperwork, not the laboratory.** A requisition for four hundred
  muzzles is worse than a scene of the muzzles being fitted, and it is cheaper to
  make.

bank.** Six root categories, each with named variants, every entry traceable to a
root. Twenty-four entries so no card feels like the eighty-third copy of
something.

Branch assignment is fixed here and drives which keywords a card may roll.

### SEUCHE — the cultures

**A closed loop worth knowing before you write any of these.** Accounts files
Procedure Seuche under *agricultural research* to avoid the disclosure
requirements attached to medical research (Volume IV §2). So **the Office's own
paperwork is full of food-production vocabulary** — starters, proofing, spoilage
— for budget reasons.

And the troops independently named these things after bread, because of the
smell.

**Neither side knows the other is using the same words.** The accidental-truth
rule operating across an entire branch.

| Designation | Troops call it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Muster 2 | **starters** | Culture stock. The Office means it exactly as a baker would. |
| Muster 4 | **bakers** | The root. Spatial, slow, smells of bread. Not a duel — a room becoming unavailable. |
| Muster 4/a | **early shift** | First-wave. Sent ahead to make ground unusable before anything arrives to use it. |
| Muster 5s | **proofing** | Held stock, not yet issued. Warehoused. |
| Muster 7 | **the risen** | **Read this note before writing one.** Troops think it means risen from the dead, and now they are sometimes right. The Office means it as dough rises, because that is the vocabulary its filing forced on it — and the Office is also correct, some of the time, about the other meaning. **Neither side can tell which from the paperwork alone**, and the Office has never found this worth resolving, because resolving it would mean admitting the two procedures are related. The ambiguity is the joke now, not the misunderstanding — never resolve it in either direction. |
| Muster 6 | **the quiet ones** | Late-stage, fully driven. **No bread smell** — that is how you know it is a six. |
| Muster 6/b | **the very quiet** | Further along. Nobody has established whether there is a limit. |
| — | **spoilage** | Not a designation. Accounts' word for all of the above once it stops performing to projection, and the men picked it up. |

### For the generator

Twenty-four nouns: Kadaver 8, Bestiarium 8, Seuche 8. Each card is a modifier
plus one of these, and the designation is available as flavour or as a second
line where the schema allows it.

**Where a troop name is already plural** — *the litter*, *the risen*, *the quiet
ones*, *proofing*, *spoilage* — it should not be pluralised again by a modifier.
Handle it or drop those from modifier slots that assume a singular.
