# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME IV: WERK NACHTIGALL

*Reichsamt für Angewandte Lebensforschung* — the Imperial Office for Applied
Life Research. Companion to Volume III. Same standard: built once, in detail,
used by every game.

**Rewritten a second time, 28 Aug 2026.** The first rewrite added mechas,
jets, werewolves and necromancy but kept the faction's *structure* — Section
Six, filing categories, requisitions, the estimates cycle — as the thing the
volume actually spent its time on. Dan's verdict on the result, in full:
*"seriously, nothing about paperwork in the game. it's grimdark historical
fantasy."* This is that correction. **The Office administers what follows.
It does not narrate itself administering it.** A clerk's office is a fact
about the setting, stated once. It is not a lens, a running joke, or a
section heading, and nothing below is built to make it one.

**Constitution rules 1–3 and 9 still govern absolutely.** Die Ostordnung,
the camps, and every real atrocity referent below stay material, documented,
and never explained by anything supernatural.

---

## 1. What Hollernbruch is

A requisitioned sanatorium east of the Oder, on the marsh. Grey water,
grey stone, wire past the treeline. What the Reich builds there is real
because its makers believed, with a chaired professor's total confidence,
that the science was there — for the scalpel and for the rite alike, one
discipline held by men who saw no seam between a séance and a surgical
theatre.

**Three things come out of that belief, and they are the volume.**

**Iron.** Mechas — towering, riveted, oil-fed — walk on Prussian
engineering's confidence that scale solves what elegance cannot. Panzer
built heavier than anything across the wire. Jets the Reich should not have
yet and does. They fail in the field, often and badly — a joint seizes, a
downed hull cannot be opened from inside — and the war goes on around the
ones that hold.

**Flesh.** The Bestiarium grafts men into hybrids where surgery and the
occult stop being separable acts. What comes out has a shape the enemy has
one word for. It is grafted, awake, and it remembers what it was.

**The dead.** Seuche raises them. Not a misunderstanding, not a metaphor —
bodies get up. The rite is performed with the same flat confidence a surgeon
brings to an incision, and nothing about it is played as awe by the men who
do it, which is exactly why it is horrifying to watch.

*Real referent, and it outranks the fiction: the medical experiments at
Ravensbrück and Dachau, the racial-hygiene programme that supplied their
justification, and Aktion T4's murder of disabled people. Ours is invention,
dressed in the confidence the real programme actually held. Theirs was
documented, and worse.*

## 2. What it is like to face them

A Gestell frame does not run. It walks through what is in front of it —
a fence, a wall, a man — at a speed that never varies, and the only mercy
in that is that you can hear it coming from a long way off.

A hybrid soldier moves like the animal it was grafted from, not like a man
imitating one. It is fast in a way training cannot produce and does not
tire the way a man tires.

The risen do not scream and do not stop. A wound that would put a living
man down does not put down something Seuche already reclaimed once.

None of it is fair, and none of it is meant to be. **They do not win
engagements. They refuse to lose ground**, and the frame, the hybrid, the
risen soldier are all built for exactly that — presence that will not
withdraw, at any cost the Reich is willing to spend, and the Reich has
never yet found the cost too high.

---

## 3. The thing they cannot have

**This is the faction's engine.** The Office knows the other two
programmes have something it does not — an Allied apparatus that bends
distance, a Japanese programme whose subjects do things no chemistry and
no rite of the Office's own accounts for.

In March 1943 the Office obtained a fused subject: alive, taken in the
Pacific — by whom, no volume says — and brought across two theatres at
ruinous cost.

Eleven weeks, every instrument the Reich's combined confidence in surgery
and the occult could bring to bear on one body. **They found nothing.** No
organ, no gland, no structure, no residue, no resonance a rite could raise
or a blade could isolate. To the limit of what the finest surgeons and the
most confident occultists in Europe could determine: an ordinary human
being.

They did not conclude the answer is not there. **They concluded they had
not looked hard enough**, and went back to the body, and looked again —
with a séance room now standing beside the bone saw, at material that
holds neither answer.

That is the faction entire. Every discipline the Reich could bring to a
question, burned on the same body, and they cannot accept what the ash
means.

---

## 4. Die Ostordnung, and the ordering that must not invert

Werk Nachtigall is downstream of *die Ostordnung*, the Empire's programme
in the east. Always was. The camps come first. The Office uses what they
produce — material, and the precedent for producing more — from a system
that existed before the Office did and would exist without it.

**This ordering is load-bearing and never inverts.** Mechas, hybrids, the
risen — these are Werk Nachtigall's monsters. **They are not the atrocity.
They are what the atrocity was for.** A story that lets the fictional
horror upstage the real one has made the real one into set dressing, which
is the one failure this volume cannot survive.

## 5. Who runs it

- **Oberregierungsrat Klaus Brehm-Sandt**, Director. Signs off on frames,
  hybrids, and reanimation with the same pen. Fond of his daughters, and
  not a monster in any way he would recognise — which is the point, not a
  softening of it.
- **Sanitätsrat Dr Helene Aust**, Sector Physician. Has objected, formally,
  nineteen times in eight years. None of it has stopped anything, and she
  has not stopped objecting.
- **Werkmeister Otto Pflug**, Bestiarium. Runs the kennels and the hybrid
  stock, kind to animals in a way that survives everything the work does
  to him, and cannot say where the line is now that the product is
  sometimes a man.
- **The subject taken in March 1943.** Unnamed. Hyakki Yakō's people know
  one of theirs was taken and do not know where.

*Section Six exists — six clerks, second floor, feared — and it is a fact
about the Office's structure, stated here once. It is not the volume's
subject and no writer should make it the subject of a scene.*

## 6. Open, deliberately

- Whether Brehm-Sandt has ever privately understood what he administers.
- Who the March 1943 subject was.
- Whether Aust's objections have ever reached anyone who could act on them.
- Whether the Office's occult sections believe what they do works, or have
  simply never been in a position to find out — the same uncertainty that
  already governs Hyakki Yakō's ceremony, arrived at independently.
