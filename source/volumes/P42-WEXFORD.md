# PROJECT 42 — COLONEL NORMAN WEXFORD

**Fork A.** The man who holds position 1 and means it completely.

---

## 1. Played straight, and that is a craft instruction

**Wexford is not a villain, not a fool, and not secretly cynical.** He is a
competent regular officer running an impossible installation, and he is written
dead straight so that the comedy everywhere else in the faction has something to
land against.

If a scene makes Wexford absurd, the scene is wrong. **He is the only person at
Camp Iron Bell who is never funny.**

---

## 2. What he believes

> *"Every person here was going to die, or vanish, or be forgotten. We are the
> only reason any of them still exist. Name the harm."*

He means it, and the strongest version of it is not sentimental: a retrieval only
closes if the century already lost the subject, so **by construction every
settled person was already gone.** He is not taking futures from people. He is
taking people out of a hole the century had already dug.

**He also believes the programme is steering history.**

It is not. The Consistency Finding says the loop is already closed and
everything Camp Iron Bell has ever done was always already done. **Wexford knows
the Finding. He has never connected it to himself**, and nobody has forced him
to, and the game never forces him either.

**This is the faction's central irony and it is never stated to him or to the
player.** §00.

---

## 3. Nineteen

Bright thinks the limit on retrievals is money, and she is right.

**Wexford has never once thought of it that way.**

The programme is nineteen people because nineteen people is the number he can
protect — the number whose names he knows, whose faces he passes every morning,
whose safety he has personally staked himself on. He would be appalled to hear
it argued as arithmetic, and he would not be able to answer the arithmetic if
someone made him try.

**Bright is right about the numbers and wrong about him.** Both halves of that
are load-bearing and neither cancels the other.

---

## 4. What he actually does all day

Not physics. **The camp.**

He signs off on every retrieval personally and reads every case himself before
he does. He has refused four in eighteen months, always on the subject's own
condition, never on cost, and every refusal costs him a bad night.

He walks the Annex on Thursdays. He does not join the card game and has never
been invited, and he arranges his walk so that he passes while it is happening.

He has written nineteen letters — one per settled person — to be opened in the
event of his death, and Prentiss knows they exist because she typed the
envelopes and has never asked what is in them.

---

## 5. The two things he will not do

**He will not send anyone back.** Retrieval is one-way as physics; keeping them
at Iron Bell afterward is a policy, and it is his. He has been asked twice — once
by Ferrer, to his face — and refused both times without raising his voice either
time, which is somehow worse than if he had.

**He will not discuss the county.** The two fountains outside Building 1, the
billeting denials, the ordinance signs. He does what the state requires of him
and he does not defend it, explain it, or apologise for it, and he has never
once been heard to say anything about it in any direction.

**That silence is the most damning thing about him**, and he has never found
the contradiction interesting enough to notice.

---

## 6. Playing him

- **Never write him as steering.** Write him as *believing he is steering*, which
  is a different performance and a sadder one.
- **He is generous, personally.** He learns names, he remembers illnesses, he is
  good at the part of command that is about people. That is precisely why
  nineteen feels to him like a family and not a figure.
- **He never explains the Finding.** He can. He knows it cold. He does not
  connect it to himself, and a scene that has him do so is the wrong scene.
- **Bright has never said this to his face, and she is not going to.** Not fear
  — she is not frightened of him. She has thought through exactly what she would
  say and decided the saying would cost more than the silence does: if the
  programme scaled to meet the arithmetic, Iron Bell would stop being nineteen
  people small enough to know and become a population, and the thing that makes
  it bearable would be the thing she destroyed getting the number right. She
  keeps the thought and does not raise it. Never have her raise it.

---

## 7. Open, deliberately

- What is in the nineteen letters.
- Whether he has ever suspected the Finding applies to him.
- **Whether he has ever counted how many of the nineteen were not already
  lost.** Three were not. Bright, Cheung and Kell are forward-settled — their
  own century has not reached them yet, it did not lose them. His belief in §2
  covers sixteen people and he has never noticed which three it does not.
  *This is deliberate. Do not fix it, and never let him work it out.* It is the
  same blindness as the Finding, with three names attached who eat in his
  mess, and it is the one thing Bright could say to him that would actually
  land — which is exactly why she never says it.
- Why he times the Thursday walk.
