# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME XVII: THE TREATY

**Fork C.** The faction's central artefact, written out in full.

Nine articles. Referenced in five volumes and never shown. Here it is.

---

## 1. What it is

Drafted by **Onmyōji-Taisa Reiko Amatsu** over four nights in November 1941,
three months after the selection at Light Seven, at the same time as the official
account was being written in Tokyo by men who had not been present.

It is **genuinely fine legal prose.** That is not a compliment and it is not
padding — it is the point. A clumsy document would be easy to dismiss. This one
is careful, balanced, and properly constructed, and a reader can feel the
competence in it, which is what makes the thing underneath unbearable.

It lives in the third cabinet in the Director's office at Light Seven. Sergeant
Hoshino dusts it.

**Nothing has ever signed the other side of it.**

---

## 2. The document

> **INSTRUMENT OF UNDERSTANDING**
> Concluded at the Noto Station, the eleventh month of the sixteenth year of
> Shōwa.
>
> **Between** the Imperial Japanese Army, hereinafter the First Party,
> **and** the Presence attested at the Noto Station on the fourth day of the
> eighth month, hereinafter the Second Party.

---

**ARTICLE I — Recognition**

The First Party recognises the Second Party as a contracting party possessed of
its own purposes, and does not assert any claim to know them.

---

**ARTICLE II — Scope**

This Instrument governs the relations of the Parties in respect of persons
selected by the Second Party, and in no other respect. Nothing herein shall be
construed as extending to any matter not expressly provided for.

---

**ARTICLE III — Selection**

The First Party acknowledges the discretion of the Second Party in the selection
of persons, and undertakes not to impede it.

The First Party may make representations as to the suitability of a person. Such
representations are advisory.

---

**ARTICLE IV — Notification**

The First Party undertakes to maintain a record of each selection and to make
that record available to the Second Party upon request.

---

**ARTICLE V — Conduct of Selected Persons**

Selected persons remain subject to the discipline and command of the First Party
in all matters not arising from their selection.

---

**ARTICLE VI — Care of Selected Persons**

The First Party undertakes to provide for the welfare, medical attention, and
proper treatment of selected persons, and to make no use of them beyond that
consistent with their condition.

The First Party further undertakes to provide for the dependants of selected
persons, whether or not the selected person survives.

The First Party affirms that selected persons are soldiers of the Empire and are
entitled to be so regarded.

---

**ARTICLE VII — Records**

Each Party shall maintain its own record of the matters herein. Where the records
of the Parties differ, the Parties shall confer.

---

**ARTICLE VIII — Amendment**

This Instrument may be amended by written agreement of the Parties.

---

**ARTICLE IX — Entry into Force**

This Instrument shall enter into force upon signature by both Parties.

> *Signed at the Noto Station —*
>
> *For the First Party: [signed] R. Amatsu, Onmyōji-Taisa*
>
> *For the Second Party:*

---

## 3. What the document actually does

**Read the articles again in this order and the shape appears: III, then VI, then
IX.**

**Article III gives away everything.** The First Party *acknowledges the
discretion* of the Second Party and *undertakes not to impede it*. That is not a
concession extracted in negotiation. It is the Empire writing down that it cannot
stop what is already happening, in the most dignified language available, and
filing it as a term.

**Article VI is Ishida's.** He believes it protects them, and he has said so to
Mifune and to others, sincerely.

**It is an obligation the First Party undertakes toward its own people.** Every
word of it binds the Empire. Nothing in it asks the Second Party for anything,
constrains it, or mentions it. Ishida is reading a promise the Empire made to
itself and hearing a protection granted from outside.

*And it is a good article.* If it were honoured, it would matter. That is why he
believes it.

**Reconciled with Volume XV §3, which was written first and is correct:** Article
VI covers dependants as well as the selected, and **nobody has ever tested it,
because there is no procedure for raising a matter under it, because there is no
other party to raise it with.**

That sits alongside Amatsu's claim below rather than against it. *Honoured* and
*enforceable* are different words. The Empire has kept this article voluntarily
for three years and there is no mechanism by which anyone could have made it.

**And then Article IX.**

The Instrument enters into force upon signature by both Parties. The signature
block has one signature.

**The treaty says, in its own final line, that it is not in effect.**

Amatsu drafted that. She could have written *upon signature by the First Party*,
or *upon deposit*, or omitted the article entirely, and any of those would have
let the document pretend. She wrote the correct clause, the one a competent
lawyer writes, and it closes the trap she built.

**Nobody has noticed.** Ishida has read Article VI more times than anyone alive
and has never followed it four articles further.

---

## 4. Amatsu's defence, which is the strongest argument in the faction

She has never denied any of this. Asked directly — and Hoshino has come close
twice — she would say approximately this:

> An Empire that admits it is being used has no policy. A programme with no
> policy is disbanded. Disbanding this programme would not stop a single
> selection, and it would end the medical attention, the records, and the
> pensions.
>
> Article VI binds nobody but us. **It is the only article that has ever been
> honoured.**

That last line is true and it is the reason the document is not simply a lie. The
Empire has in fact provided the welfare, the medical attention, the proper
regard. **Amatsu wrote one enforceable obligation into a treaty with nobody, put
it on the only party she controlled, and then made her programme keep it.**

Whether that makes her the most honest person in the faction or the most
culpable is not resolved and will not be.

---

## 5. Article VII, which nobody discusses

> *Where the records of the Parties differ, the Parties shall confer.*

Two accounts of August 1941 sit in the same cabinet — the first-hand notes and
the November version — and they differ (Volume XV §2).

**By the treaty's own terms, that is a matter for conferral.**

Nobody has ever proposed invoking it. Hoshino has read both accounts and put them
back in the order he found them. Sakaguchi's twelfth theory does not cite Article
VII. It is simply not mentioned, by anyone, ever, and there is no instruction
anywhere that it should not be.

> *Not in the file: whether anyone has tried.*

---

## 6. Using it

- **The treaty is quotable.** Any character may cite an article. Ishida cites VI.
  Amatsu cites nothing.
- **Never have a character explain the Article IX problem.** A player or reader
  finds it by reading to the end, which takes ninety seconds and is the best
  ninety seconds this faction has.
- **Do not let the Second Party ever respond**, to this or to anything. No sign,
  no acknowledgement, no answer to Ishida's clarification.
- **The document is beautiful and that is load-bearing.** Do not degrade it, do
  not add crossings-out, do not make it look mad. It is the work of a competent
  officer doing her job well.

---

## 7. Open, deliberately

- Whether Amatsu wrote Article IX knowing, or wrote it correctly by habit and
  understood afterward.
- Whether Ishida's clarification, if it were ever answered, would be answered
  under Article VIII.
- What conferral under Article VII would look like.
