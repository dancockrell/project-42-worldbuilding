# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME XVI: WHAT IS CAMP IRON BELL ENTITLED TO DO?

The seven positions, who holds them, and why each is defensible.

**Written for The Long Night's six protagonists**, who each answer this
differently, and for any P42 scene where somebody has to have a view. Domain A is
unowned; prime is covering it.

**§00 governs. The game never picks.** If a scene resolves this, the scene is
wrong.

---

## How to use this

Every position below is **held by somebody the reader can respect**, and every
one has a counter that is also good. That is the test — if a position can only be
held by a fool or a monster, it has been written badly and should be rewritten
until an intelligent person could arrive at it honestly.

**Nobody in the universe holds one purely.** People hold one publicly, a second
privately, and drift toward a third at four in the morning.

---

## 1. It is a rescue

> *"Every person here was going to die, or vanish, or be forgotten. We are the
> only reason any of them still exist. Name the harm."*

**Held by: Colonel Wexford**, and he means it completely.

The strongest version is not sentimental. A retrieval only closes if the record
already lost the subject — so **by construction, every settled person was
already gone.** The programme does not take futures away from people. It takes
people out of a hole the century had already dug.

**The counter:** rescue implies the rescued get to leave afterward. Nobody has
ever been sent home. Nineteen people live in married quarters nobody is married
in, doing war work for a country most of them were not born in, permanently.
**There is a word for saving somebody into your own service, and it is not
rescue.**

---

## 2. It is a conscription

> *"I did not drown. I also did not volunteer. Those are two different facts and
> this programme has never once written down the second one."*

**Held by: Ensign Tobias Ferrer**, and he has never let it go.

Nobody signs anything. The subject is unconscious, disoriented, or dead-by-record
at the moment of the decision, and the decision is made by three signatures none
of which are theirs. **W.D. 42-A has a box for the authorising officer and no box
for consent**, because the form was designed by people for whom the question did
not arise.

**The counter:** consent from whom, and when? You cannot ask a man in 1799
whether he consents to being retrieved in 1943 — the question is not merely
impractical, it is incoherent. **A standard nobody could ever meet is not a
standard, it is a refusal dressed as one.**

---

## 3. The criteria are the crime

> *"Not that we take. Who we can take."*

**Held by: Nkemdirim Osei**, quietly, and by Miriam Halloran-Sze before him.

The programme can only reach people history was careless with. In 1888
Whitechapel that is the casual poor. Across the roster it is people the record
neglected **because of who they were** — and Volume VII says the programme has
never written that observation down, because writing it down would require
somebody to have made it.

**The sharpest form**, and nobody at Iron Bell has said it aloud: **the programme
benefits from people going uncounted.** A social investigator who succeeds makes
a subject unreachable. The programme's interests and the century's indifference
point the same direction.

**The counter:** this indicts a constraint, not a choice. Nobody at Iron Bell
selected the criteria; the physics did. **Blaming a programme for the shape of
what it can reach is like blaming a net for the size of its holes** — and the
alternative to a compromised rescue is not a clean one, it is none.

---

## 4. It does not matter, because nothing changes

> *"The Finding is not a limitation. It is the whole answer, and everyone here is
> refusing to hear it."*

**Held by: Marta Kell**, and she is the only person at Iron Bell who states it
plainly.

The loop is already closed. Whatever the programme did, it had always already
done. There is no branch where a different decision was taken, **so there was
never a decision.** The moral debate at Camp Iron Bell is nineteen people arguing
about a choice the universe has already recorded.

**The counter, and it is the best one in the volume:** *knowing* the loop is
closed does not tell you what is in it. **Somebody still has to decide, and the
deciding is real to them, and the record will contain whatever they do.** A
fatalist still has to fill in the form. Kell's position explains everything and
excuses nothing, and she knows it, which is why she is the way she is.

---

## 5. It is owed

> *"We were handed the most extraordinary capability in human history in the
> middle of the worst war in human history. Declining to use it is not
> conscience. It is squeamishness with better vocabulary."*

**Held by: Major Vosburgh**, who runs the camp and has to make it work.

This is the administrator's position and it is not stupid. Men are dying at a
rate of thousands a day. The programme could shorten that. **Every hour spent on
whether it is entitled to is an hour of the actual war**, and the people arguing
are largely people the programme rescued.

**The counter:** it proves too much. "There is a war on" has authorised every
atrocity in the volume next to this one, and the Office at Hollernbruch would
recognise the argument word for word. **When your justification is available
verbatim to Werk Nachtigall, check it.**

---

## 6. The question is a distraction

> *"Nobody has asked who it is for."*

**Held by: Ida Prentiss**, who has no clearance and no opinion on the ethics, and
who has read every piece of paper that has crossed the camp in eighteen months.

Follow the appropriations. Who authorised the programme, against what budget,
expecting what return? Nineteen people at ruinous cost, in a war being decided by
infantry and logistics and ships. **The capability has not shortened the war by a
day and the funding has never been questioned**, and that is a fact about
somebody's intentions.

**The counter:** she is describing an institution, not answering the question. A
programme can be badly motivated and still be doing right, or well motivated and
doing wrong. **Following the money tells you who benefits. It does not tell you
what is permitted.**

---

## 7. We should be taking far more

> *"You are all debating the ethics of a rowing boat while the ship goes down.
> If this is a rescue, rescue more. If it is not, stop pretending."*

**Held by: Corporal Adaeze Bright**, and this is the position nobody expects and
the hardest to answer.

The limit on retrievals is **cost**. Not ethics, not law, not consent — power
budget and estimates. Nineteen people, because twenty was expensive. **If the
programme is doing right, then the number is an accountant's decision about how
much right to do**, which is obscene. If it is doing wrong, the debate is
theatre.

**The counter:** scale changes the act. Nineteen displaced people is a strange
household. Nineteen thousand is a population removed from history, and the
Consistency Finding does not obviously survive it — nobody knows what happens
when the record has to accommodate absences at that scale, **and the programme's
own physicist will not say it is safe.**

Bright's answer to the counter: *"Then find out. You have a machine."*

**Nobody has taken this to Wexford. It is in a memorandum she has not sent.**

---

## For The Long Night's six

Six protagonists, six positions, and the seventh available as an antagonist or a
late turn. **Suggested assignment is not binding** — but do not give two
protagonists the same position, and **do not give anyone the position the game
agrees with**, because there is not one.

The strongest party has **at least one person holding 4 or 7**, because those are
the two that make the other five uncomfortable, and a party where everyone is
uncomfortable in a different direction is a party with scenes in it.

---

## Open, deliberately

- Whether Wexford has ever privately held any position but 1.
- What is in Bright's unsent memorandum.
- Whether Prentiss is right about the funding, and who would know.
