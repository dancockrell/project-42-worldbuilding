# PROJECT 42 — SOUND SPECIFICATION

**Volume XIII.** Executable form of LORE-BIBLE.md §7, which stated the direction
in one section while art got a whole document. That imbalance was inside my own
lane and this closes it.

Nothing here has been produced. **Recorded as unbuilt rather than left to look
finished**, same standard as ART-SPEC §8.

---

## 1. The governing idea

**Everything is recorded, not performed.**

The soundtrack behaves like it is coming off period media — wire recorder,
shellac, shortwave — with the artefacts that implies: limited bandwidth, wow and
flutter, surface noise, a noise floor that is never silent.

**Then the superhuman elements arrive in full modern fidelity, and that contrast
is the horror.**

This is the same seam as the art's two-layer rule (ART-SPEC §3), in another
sense, and it fails the same way: **do not blend them.** A period-filtered
supernatural effect is a wasted opportunity. The point is that one layer sounds
like 1944 and the other sounds like *now*, and the join is audible.

### The test

If a listener cannot tell that two different recording eras are stacked, the cue
failed. Regenerate rather than accept — "close enough" is what turns a distinctive
soundtrack into generic war-game ambience.

---

## 2. The hum — the setting's single most identifiable sound

**Sixty cycles, everywhere Camp Iron Bell appears.** The wire, the huts, the
bedframes (Volume III §2).

This is not ambience. It is a **narrative instrument**, and it does one job no
other sound in the project can do:

> **The hum dropping out means a frame has taken the load.** Something is about
> to happen or has just failed.

Personnel stop hearing it in the second month and therefore cannot hear its
absence either. **The player will do the same**, which is why it works: play it
long enough that the listener stops noticing, then take it away.

**Implementation rules:**

- Present in every Iron Bell scene except the mess hall.
- **The mess hall is acoustically dead.** Thick brick, no conduit. It should be
  noticeably, uncomfortably quiet, and that is why men eat there at eleven at
  night when they are not hungry.
- Never fade the hum out. **Cut it.** A fade is a mix decision; a cut is an event.
- Nothing should call attention to it. No character remarks on it. Ever.

## 3. Per-faction sonic identity

### Project 42

**Base:** big band and swing, *degrading* — tape stretch, pitch drift, a
recording that has been played too many times. Never pristine. The music is
always a copy of a copy.

**Bed:** the hum (§2). Plus, in the Line Shed, oil under load — a low note that
**climbs a fifth** when a frame takes power. That interval is the camp's warning
system and should be consistent enough to learn.

**Effects layer:** Tesla arcs as percussion. Bright, clean, cyan-sounding if a
sound can be. **Optimistic-sounding power**, matching the art direction — the
horror in this faction is never in the presentation.

**Signature device — reversed audio.** Tails that precede their transients. A
radio voice that answers before the question. Use sparingly and never explain it;
time travel as texture, not as a plot point.

**Retrieval sequence**, and it is the faction's set piece: the hum cuts, the oil
note climbs, then **nine to forty minutes compressed into whatever the scene
allows** — mostly waiting. The arrival is not a bang. It is gradual and sideways.
Something becoming true rather than appearing.

Then, always, **the buckets.** The most tightly held operational fact in the
programme is that its miracle is undignified, and sound is the only medium that
can deliver that without showing it.

### Werk Nachtigall

**Base:** military brass band on failing instruments. Splitting reeds, sticking
valves, a band that is still playing correctly and cannot.

**Bed:** pneumatics, industrial rhythm, a machine shop that never stops. Metal on
concrete. Something being wheeled.

**And wetness.** This is the faction's whole sound-design problem in one word:
it is mechanical *and* organic, so **put a wet sound where a metal one belongs.**
A hydraulic that sounds like a swallow. A hinge that sounds soft.

**Rule 4 reversed in audio, 28 Aug 2026 — bible §2 rule 4.** The prior hard
rule forbade any occult register outright. Dan's ruling reverses it: chant,
drone, and ritual sound are now available to Werk Nachtigall, and they do
not replace the factory bed, they sit inside it. **A low chant under the
machine-shop rhythm, not instead of it.** A ritual cue is scored with the
same wet/mechanical mixing rule as everything else in this faction (below)
— it should still sound like it was built and operated, not summoned.
**What has not changed:** nothing here is ever scored as awe, reverence,
or triumphant dark magic. The register stays flat and procedural even
when the sound is a chant — a choir that sounds bored, a ritual bell that
sounds like a factory bell, because to the Office it is administratively
the same kind of noise.

**Per branch:**
- *Kadaver* — surgical steel, trays, a theatre that is well-built and busy.
- *Bestiarium* — kennels. And **the whistle** (§5).
- *Seuche* — no longer almost nothing. Air and spores stay, but under the
  reversed rule the raised dead get a cue too: low, flat, administrative
  — closer to a machine idling than to a voice. **The ambiguity between
  cultured and raised stock (bible §2 rule 4, WORLD-BESTIARY.md) should be
  audible as well as textual: the same cue should plausibly be either.**

### Hyakki Yakō

**Base:** taiko, shakuhachi, biwa, **played properly and well.** Do not degrade
these, do not detune them, do not make them ominous. They are a real musical
tradition performed with skill, and that dignity is the point — the same argument
as ART-SPEC §1a: their own face outwards, never the enemy's version of it.

**The horror is entirely in the space.** The reverb tail is too long and belongs
to a room far larger than any room present. A shakuhachi played in a tent should
return an interval from somewhere with a ceiling three hundred feet up.

**Silence is the weapon.** The faction's power moment is **the sound cutting
out** — everything dropping except one thing that should not be audible at all.
That is "being watched" rendered in audio and it beats any sting ever written.

**The watching, as a sound rule:** it never intensifies and never relents (Volume
V). **There is no motif for it.** Do not write one. If the player can hum the
watcher's theme, the watcher has become a character, and it is not one.

## 4. UI — the interface is paper

**This is a game about paperwork committing crimes. The interface should sound
like it.**

- Card plays: **paper on wood.** Not a whoosh, not a magic chime.
- Menus: a filing cabinet. Drawers, runners, a catch.
- Confirm: a stamp. Denial: **the same stamp.** They sound identical, because on
  the desk they are.
- Errors: nothing dramatic. A drawer that does not close.

**The diegetic rule applies to sound** (bible: everything the player sees is
diegetic and may be wrong). A satisfied *approved* chime on a form that
understates the truth is the interface lying in another channel, and that is
correct and intended.

## 5. The whistle

Volume VIII: Baureihe 7 answer a whistle, and **the horror is not the thing
coming at you — it is that it was sent.**

Sound carries this better than any other medium, and it is the single highest-value
cue in the project:

- **The whistle arrives before the enemy is visible.** Off-screen, around a
  corner, down a corridor the player cannot see.
- **Never show the handler.** Ghost Front has this as a test rather than a note.
  The whistle with nobody behind it *is* the faction.
- It should be a real trench whistle — thin, cheap, unremarkable. **Not
  frightening on its own.** It becomes frightening entirely by association, which
  means the first time a player hears it, nothing should happen.

That last point is a production instruction: **spend a whistle early, for free,
with no consequence.** Everything after is paid for by it.

## 7. Practical

- Period authefacts are **processing, not source.** Record clean and degrade it —
  wow, flutter, bandwidth limiting, surface noise. A genuinely bad recording is
  just bad; a clean recording made to sound old is controllable.
- **Check licences before downloading any sample library or model.** Dan builds
  commercially. Same rule as ART-SPEC §7 and it has already caught two models.
- The modern-fidelity layer should be *conspicuously* clean. Full bandwidth, no
  noise floor. The gap is the effect.
- **Test on laptop speakers.** A 60Hz hum is inaudible on most of them, so the
  cut-out will land on nobody. Either shift the fundamental up or carry the
  information in a harmonic that survives a small driver — and **verify by
  listening on a bad speaker**, not by looking at a spectrum analyser.

That last one is ART-SPEC's "check at 300px" rule in audio: **test the artefact
at the size it will actually be experienced.**
