# PROJECT 42 — THE BRIGHT MEMORANDUM

**Fork A / Project 42.** The document named but not shown in *What Is Camp Iron
Bell Entitled To Do?* §7.

Corporal Adaeze Bright wrote it in January 1944. It is four pages. **She has not
sent it**, and it is in the same folder as her requisition denials, which she
keeps and says are for the memoir.

Position 7 is described there as the one nobody expects and the hardest to
answer. This is why.

---

## 1. The document

> **MEMORANDUM**
> To: Commanding Officer, Camp Iron Bell
> From: Cpl A. Bright, Current Section
> Subject: **Retrieval throughput — request for review of the limiting factor**
> Classification: as the Colonel prefers. I have not marked it.

---

**1.** This memorandum concerns the number nineteen.

**2.** I have been here ten months. In that time the programme has completed
four retrievals. The limiting factor in every case was the power budget, and
the power budget is limited by the Line Shed's plant, and the plant is limited
by an appropriation. **At no point in the chain does the limit arise from
physics, law, medicine, or the Consistency Finding.** It arises from money.

**3.** I want to be precise, because I expect this to be read by somebody
looking for a reason to file it. **I am not alleging that the programme is
underfunded.** I am observing that if the programme is doing right, then the
quantity of right it does is currently set by an appropriations officer who has
not been told what the appropriation is for.

**4.** I have seen the estimates. A retrieval costs approximately what four
medium tanks cost. The Army will build four medium tanks this afternoon.

**5.** The counter-argument I anticipate is scale: that nineteen displaced
persons is a manageable anomaly and nineteen thousand is not, and that the
Finding may not accommodate absences at that volume.

**6. I accept that this is a real question. I do not accept that it has been
asked.** No study exists. No estimate exists. Mr Osei has not been tasked with
it and has told me he would take it if he were. **The position that we cannot
is being held by people who have not checked whether we can**, and that is not
caution, it is the appearance of caution, which is cheaper.

**7.** I am aware how this memorandum reads. I am aware that I am the person on
this establishment least able to write it and most likely to be read as
ungrateful for having been written down at all.

**8.** I would ask the Colonel to consider that the two facts are related.
Everyone here who might raise this is either an officer whose career depends on
the programme continuing as it is, or one of the settled, who are guests. **The
only people with standing to ask are the people with the most to lose by
asking.**

**9.** My request is narrow. **Commission the study.** Task Mr Osei. Find out
what the Finding tolerates. If the answer is nineteen, I will never raise this
again and the Colonel may put this memorandum in nine.

**10.** If the answer is not nineteen, then we have been letting people stay
where they fell in order to underspend a line item, and somebody should have to
write that down.

> *A. Bright, Cpl.*
> *Not submitted.*

---

## 2. Why it has not been sent

**Not fear.** She is not frightened of Wexford and it would be a misreading to
write her that way.

She has not sent it because **she does not know what happens to her if it is
approved**, and she has thought about that more carefully than anyone would
expect.

If the programme scales, the settled stop being a household of nineteen and
become a population. **The Annex stops being married quarters with curtains and
becomes accommodation.** The thing that makes Iron Bell bearable — that it is
small enough for everyone to be a person in it — is the same thing she is asking
to destroy.

She knows this. It is paragraph 7 in a version she wrote in December and cut,
and the cut is why the memorandum is four pages rather than five.

---

## 3. What makes it unanswerable

**Every counter to Bright's position concedes something worse.**

- *We cannot afford more.* Then the number of people saved is an accounting
  outcome.
- *The Finding may not tolerate it.* Then commission the study. **There isn't
  one.**
- *Scale changes the act.* Agreed — and that is an argument for deciding
  deliberately, not for arriving at nineteen by inertia.
- *It is not your place.* Paragraph 8 answers this in advance, and answers it
  by pointing out that the objection is structural rather than personal.

**Wexford's honest answer, if he ever read it, is the one he cannot give:** that
the programme is nineteen people because nineteen people is what he can protect,
and that he has never once thought of it as a budget and would be appalled to see
it written as one, and that Bright is right about the arithmetic and wrong about
him.

---

## 4. Using it

- **Nobody has read this.** If a game shows it, it is being found rather than
  received.
- **Bright does not talk about it.** She does not hint. She keeps denials, not
  drafts, and this is filed with the denials, which is its own comment.
- **Never have Wexford read it on screen.** The interesting version is the one
  where he never does.
- **Paragraph 10 is the line.** *Somebody should have to write that down.*
