# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME XVI: THE FUSED

**Fork C.** Hyakki Yakō's people, named. Companion to Volume V (premise) and
Volume XV (the place).

**Read the guard in LORE-BIBLE.md §5 before adding to this file.** These are
people. Dispossession, never frenzy. The failure mode here is not a continuity
error.

---

## 1. What this roster has to do

**Forty-one, as of March 1944.** Forty-two if the woman in Kanazawa is counted,
and the programme has never decided whether she is.

The roster's job is not to list them. It is to **demonstrate that the selection
has no pattern**, and it does that by defeating every theory a reader constructs
while reading it.

The programme has produced eleven theories in three years. **Every one of them
had to account for the cook, and none of them did.** They are all still in the
cabinet.

So: read for the pattern. There is not one. That is the volume.

### The one rule the programme is certain of

**It never takes two people from the same room twice.**

Eleven were in the signal hut in August 1941. It took four. It has never come
back to Light Seven, and the station has quietly stopped being a posting people
fear and started being one they request.

Nobody has any idea whether this means anything.

## 2. The named

### Tsuruoka Masaru — cook, selected August 1941

He came in about the stove.

No rank worth recording, no aptitude anyone had noticed, no belief, no reason to
be in the room. He is the programme's permanent problem and its most honest fact,
and he is the reason all eleven theories are wrong.

**What he can do has never been satisfactorily written down**, because the
officer assigned to write it down watched him for six weeks and produced two
sentences. Food he has cooked does not spoil. Men who eat at his table sleep
without dreaming, and say so, and cannot be got to elaborate.

He was offered a commission in 1942 and declined it. The refusal sits in his file
with no annotation, which means somebody decided not to comment and that decision
was itself not commented on.

**He still cooks.** He has asked for nothing in three years except a better
stove, twice, and been refused both times on cost.

> *Not in the file: whether he understood, at any point, that he had a choice.*

---

### Iwasaki Chiyo — the woman in Kanazawa

**The programme calls her "the Kanazawa case." This document uses her name,
because she has one.**

Selected in 1942, by the programme's reckoning. Never inducted, never trained,
never told. She is thirty-four, she repairs and re-weaves damaged kimono for a
shop that has almost no stock left, and she has been visited twice a year since
by an officer who brings a form and asks four questions and leaves.

The four questions are: whether she has been sleeping. Whether she has been
unwell. Whether anything unusual has occurred. Whether she wishes to report
anything.

**She answers no to all four, every time, and she has been lying since the second
visit.**

She worked out what she is somewhere in 1943, from nothing but the questions —
because the questions are the wrong questions to ask a civilian, and she is not
stupid, and nobody sends an officer twice a year about sleep. She has never said
so. She has a theory about what the fourth question is for and she is correct.

**Nobody has authorised telling her. Nobody has authorised stopping the visits.**
So a lieutenant comes, twice a year, and asks a woman four questions she long
ago worked out the shape of, and she says no, and he writes *no*, and they both
go on with it.

> *Not in the file: what she would say if he asked a fifth.*

### Petty Officer Hoshino Kenji — Shrine-Keeper, selected August 1941

Dusts the treaty. Knows exactly what he is doing while he does it.

One of the four from the signal hut. **He is the only one of the four who has
read both accounts** — the first-hand notes and the official version — and he
read them in the same afternoon, in the same cabinet, and put them back in the
order he found them.

He has never told Amatsu that he read them. Amatsu has never asked. **They have
had eleven conversations since and both of them have been careful, and neither
has said about what.**

*Voice:* precise, courteous, and impossible to draw out. He will answer any
question asked and volunteer nothing whatsoever.

> *Not in the file: which account he believes.*

---

### Radioman Second Class Ueda Sanae — Fog, selected August 1941

Nineteen in 1941. Twenty-two now, and the youngest of the four.

She was at the set when it happened and had her headphones on, and **hers is the
only first-hand account that describes a sound.** The other three describe being
looked at. She describes a carrier tone that was not on any frequency the set
could receive, arriving before the selection and stopping after it.

The programme has tested this exhaustively and found nothing, and her account is
still in the cabinet, and the November version does not mention it.

She has stopped bringing it up. She has not stopped listening.

*Voice:* young, direct, and increasingly the only one of them who still asks
questions out loud. The others have learned not to. She has not, and Amatsu has
twice declined to have her taught.

> *Not in the file: whether the tone was there before August 1941 and nobody was
> listening.*

---

### Sergeant Mifune Kaoru — karakasa, selected 1942

He volunteered. He is one of the few who did and was taken, and what he got was
the smallest folklore in the catalogue.

**He guards a door.** He is genuinely, measurably good at it — nothing has passed
him in two years — and that is the humiliation, because he asked for something
enormous and received something useful.

There is no ceremony for a disappointing fusion. **One was held anyway**, in
full, correctly, and he stood through it.

Ishida told him Article 6 protects them. He believed it once. He has not said
whether he still does, and Ishida has not noticed the difference.

> *Not in the file: what he asked for.*

---

### Superior Private Kurosawa Ren — Oni, selected 1943

The drummer. The letter in `lore-pages-samples.md` is his and was never sent.

He does not remember agreeing to the drum. There is a gap between volunteering
and the ceremony the shape of a held breath, and he has never been able to fill
it in.

**He is the only fused person on the roster who has an ending available to him**,
and he knows the shape of it: there is a rhythm that makes the others fight
harder, and there is a rhythm after which there will not be enough of him left to
need the drum back.

He has played the first one nine times. He has not played the second.

*Voice:* warm, funny in company, and writes letters he does not send.

> *Not in the file: how many are in the drawer.*

---

### Chief Petty Officer Andō Michi — Fog, selected 1942

A fisherman from Wajima, forty-one, taken with her brother in the same week.

**Her brother was not selected.** He was in the boat. She was in the boat. It
took her.

He requested and received a transfer to the programme as unchosen support staff,
and he has been at Light Seven for two years, and they have not discussed it once
in that time. They work the same shift. They eat at the same table. **They are
perfectly friendly** and there is a subject the size of a house that neither has
approached from any direction.

> *Not in the file: which of them is avoiding it.*

---

### Lieutenant Sakaguchi Tomo — Shrine, selected 1942

A schoolteacher before the war and still one in every way that matters. Runs the
programme's only genuine attempt at systematic record-keeping about the fused —
what they can do, when it started, whether it changes.

**She is compiling the twelfth theory** and she already knows it will fail, and
she is compiling it anyway, because eleven failures is data and twelve is more
data.

She is the closest thing the faction has to a scientist and she is not one, and
she is aware of the difference, which is more than the programme's actual
scientists have managed.

> *Not in the file: what she will do when the twelfth fails.*

---

### The one taken in March 1943

**Name withheld here on purpose.** Volume IV's Office had them for eleven weeks
and never wrote a name down, and this document is not going to supply what the
Office declined to.

Hyakki Yakō carries them as missing, presumed lost in the Pacific. **The
programme does not know where they are and has not stopped looking.**

Sakaguchi's file on them is the thickest in the cabinet and consists almost
entirely of the search.

> *Not in the file, and not in any file: that the watcher's attention on them
> never lapsed for one moment of the eleven weeks. Nobody in the world knows
> this. No character may discover it (Volume X, hub edges).*

## 3. The unchosen

**Roughly two hundred men and women volunteered and were never selected.**

They stayed. They form the support element, they attend the ceremonies of the
people who *were* chosen, and by every account in the files they are the most
loyal personnel the programme has.

**Being passed over by something that will not say why does not read as a
reprieve to the person it happens to.**

Andō's brother is one of them. So is a man who has attended forty-one ceremonies
and has stopped counting out loud.

---

## 4. Writing them

- **Stillness, never frenzy.** The calm is the horror.
- **They do not discuss it with each other.** They can tell who else has it, and
  they leave it alone, the way people leave a shared illness alone.
- **Some have started addressing it.** Not aloud. Not asking for anything. Just
  noting things to it. This is in no report and is in several letters.
- **They do not thank the programme either.** Volume VII records the same of the
  settled, and neither institution has remarked on it.
- **Never resolve why any of them.** The roster's function is the absence of a
  pattern. A reader who finds one has been failed by whoever wrote the entry.
