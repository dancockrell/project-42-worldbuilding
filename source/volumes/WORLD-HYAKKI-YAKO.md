# PROJECT 42 — VOLUME V: HYAKKI YAKŌ

The Empire's programme. Companion to Volumes III and IV.

**Read the guard in LORE-BIBLE.md §5 before writing a line of this faction.** The
condition described here belongs to *the fused*, inside one programme. It is
never a property of Japanese people, and the caricature it sits one careless step
from was manufactured industrially by Allied propaganda in exactly the period
whose visual idiom we are working in.

---

## 1. What happened, as far as anyone can establish

In August 1941, at a research station on the Noto peninsula, an experiment in
something else entirely produced a result nobody had designed for.

The programme's official account describes a deliberate contact, a set of
protocols, and an agreement. **The official account was written in November 1941,
three months afterward, by men who had not been present.**

What the surviving first-hand notes describe is shorter and worse. Eleven people
were in the building. Something looked at them. It selected four, and the
selection took no time at all and involved no communication of any kind.

Of the four, none had any seniority. One was a cook.

### The treaty

There is a treaty. It runs to nine articles, it is beautifully drafted, and it
is filed in the third cabinet of the Director's office.

**It is written in our hand, on our paper, and nothing has ever signed the other
side of it.** The programme drafted terms it hoped had been agreed, made it
official, and filed it, the way you do.

The treaty is the faction's central artefact and the exact parallel to Werk
Nachtigall's euphemism: **a bureaucracy lying to itself in its own paperwork, in
its own characteristic way.** The Office files an atrocity as agriculture; the
programme files a unilateral event as a bilateral agreement.

---

## 2. It selects. The programme does not.

**The programme has no demonstrated influence over who is chosen.** This is the
fact everything else grows from and the fact the programme cannot say aloud.

Selection criteria are unknown. The selected include soldiers, but also a
railway clerk, two fishermen, a schoolteacher, and a woman in Kanazawa who has
never been told why the Army visits her twice a year. There is no pattern anyone
has found — no rank, no aptitude, no lineage, no belief.

### The ceremony, which may be doing nothing at all

The programme conducts a rite before fusion. It has a form, a sequence, an
officiant, prescribed language, and a duration.

**Nobody knows if any of it matters.** Fusions have occurred without it. Fusions
have failed to occur with it, correctly performed, on candidates who wanted it
badly.

The programme continues to perform it, in full, every time, and the reason is not
that anyone believes in it. **The reason is that stopping would be an admission
that the Empire has no part in this** — that it did not achieve a contact,
negotiate a treaty, or select its own soldiers, but was simply the place where
something reached in and took whoever it wanted.

So the ceremony is performed correctly. This appears not to have mattered. The
next one is scheduled.

### The unchosen

Men volunteer for Hyakki Yakō. Most are never selected, and the programme has no
explanation to give them.

They stay. They are formed into support elements, they attend the ceremonies of
the men who *were* chosen, and they are — by every account in the files — the
most loyal personnel the programme has.

**Being passed over by something that will not say why does not read as a
reprieve to the people it happens to.**

---

## 3. Being watched

Every fused person can feel it, continuously, and has been able to since the
moment of fusion. This is not metaphor and it is not paranoia. It is the
faction's founding physical fact.

Under RQM (LORE-BIBLE.md §5), **being observed is not surveillance — it is what
makes you a fact.** Something observes them without pause, which means their
realness is on loan from a lender that has never stated terms.

**What that is actually like**, and how to write it:

- It is not frightening in the moment. It is frightening in retrospect, at
  three in the morning, when you notice you have stopped minding.
- It never intensifies and never relents. There is no dramatic moment.
- The fused do not discuss it with each other. They can tell who else has it, and
  they leave it alone, in the way people leave a shared illness alone.
- Some of them have started addressing it. Not aloud. Not asking for anything.
  Just — noting things to it. This is in no report and is in several letters.

### Dispossession, not frenzy

Write this as **stillness**. A fused soldier does terrible things and does not
own the hands doing them, and afterwards is calm, and the calm is the horror.

Never write it as fanaticism, frenzy, or ecstatic self-destruction. That is the
caricature, and it is also *less* frightening, which is the practical argument if
the ethical one does not land.

---

## 4. What it does not excuse

**Both things are true at once and neither cancels the other.**

The fused are victims of something that took them without asking and will not say
what it wants. And the programme they serve belongs to a state committing
documented atrocities in Asia — **Unit 731 is the referent and it is not softened
by anything in this document.** Vivisection, plague-bomb attacks on Chinese
cities, thousands of victims whom the staff called *logs*.

And afterward, the United States granted Shirō Ishii immunity from prosecution in
exchange for the data. **That fact belongs to Project 42's bill, not only to this
faction's** — see rule 6.

Holding both without resolving is §00 applied. A version of this faction that is
purely tragic has laundered a war crime; a version that is purely monstrous has
reproduced the caricature. Neither is available.

## 5. Personnel

- **Onmyōji-Taisa Reiko Amatsu**, commanding. The title is the programme's own
  invention — an old word for a court diviner, attached to a modern rank by
  someone who wanted the thing to sound continuous with something. **She knows
  the treaty is unsigned.** She drafted it. She would do it again, and she is not
  a fool, and her reasoning is the best argument in the faction: an Empire that
  admits it is being used has no policy, and a programme with no policy is
  disbanded, and disbanding it would not stop a single selection. **This is
  duty she holds, not duty holding her.** She is not fused. Nothing reaches
  into her the way it reaches into the people she commands, and the choice to
  keep carrying the treaty alone is hers to make and hers to answer for — the
  opposite condition from the one she gives orders to.
- **Captain Ishida**, liaison. Believes the treaty protects them. Has drafted a
  clarification and is waiting for a reply. **He will be waiting for the whole
  war.** Sincere, decent, and entirely wrong, which makes him the most useful
  viewpoint character the faction has. **His loyalty is his own, freely given
  to a document that does not deserve it** — a mistake a self-possessed person
  makes, not a symptom of anything done to him.
- **The Kanazawa woman.** Selected, never inducted, visited twice yearly by an
  officer who brings a form and asks four questions and leaves. She has never
  been told what she is. She has begun to work it out. Nobody has authorised
  telling her and nobody has authorised stopping the visits.
- **The one taken in March 1943.** Missing. Presumed lost in the Pacific. The
  programme does not know Hollernbruch has them, and has not stopped looking. See
  Volume IV §3.

## 6. The three branches

- **Fog** — the not-quite-arrived. Concealment that is not hiding: a person who
  is a fact for fewer of the people present.
- **Oni** — sacrifice. The fusion burns the host, and the host chooses when. The
  only real agency the faction has is over *when it ends*, which is a bleak thing
  to be the one choice available.
- **Shrine** — wards, cycles, the ceremony. **This is the branch that may be
  entirely decorative** (§2) and it is played completely straight. The player
  should never be told. They should notice, eventually, that nothing in the
  Shrine branch has a mechanism anyone can point to.

---

## 7. The universe's spine, visible from here

Worth recording where all three volumes can see it.

- **Project 42** cannot change anything. The Consistency Finding: the loop is
  already closed, and everything they do was always already done.
- **Werk Nachtigall** dissected the answer and found nothing in it, and keeps
  cutting because the alternative is admitting their tools cannot reach it.
- **Hyakki Yakō** performs a ceremony that may do nothing, under a treaty nothing
  signed, for something that selects whoever it likes.

**All three superpowers of this world are engaged in enormous, meticulous,
expensively documented work that may accomplish nothing at all — and none of them
can stop, because stopping would mean saying so.**

That is §00 at the scale of the setting: the question is worth more than the
answer, and nobody in this world is going to get the answer.

## 7a. The twenty-five forms are canon and live in their own file

`HY-THE-FUSED-ROSTER.md` fixes the twenty-five folklore forms, assigns each to
Fog, Oni or Shrine, and states that these are **decisions, not proposals** --
the same thing `WORLD-BESTIARY.md` §6a does for Werk Nachtigall's twenty-four.

It was separated out because it needs a guard the bestiary does not. **The
bestiary catalogues products. The roster is a list of what was done to people**,
and the moment it reads as a bestiary this faction has become the caricature.
`tools/hy-roster-check.py` keeps it in step with the card generator both ways.
