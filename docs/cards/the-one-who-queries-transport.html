THE ONE WHO QUERIES TRANSPORT

Section VI, second floor, the old children's ward. Six people work there
and nobody at Hollernbruch can name any of them, this one included — known,
when known at all, by what they sign.

In eight years this desk has queried feed variances, muzzle allocations,
and a depreciation schedule nobody else read twice. In March 1943 it
queried one transport, cost eleven hundred and forty Reichsmarks against a
line item that should have run under four hundred, and held the release
pending clarification of the discrepancy — the only objection Werk
Nachtigall's entire apparatus raised to what that transport carried, and it
was about the money.

ADEQUATE TO THE APPROPRIATION

The requisition arrived on the fourteenth with the transport cost entered
in a hand this desk did not recognise, eleven hundred and forty
Reichsmarks against an authorisation that had never, in six years of
comparable movements, exceeded four hundred, and the desk did what it did
to every anomalous figure since 1937 — held it and wrote a query, because
a number that did not reconcile was, whatever else it might also be, a
number that did not reconcile, and reconciling numbers was the whole of
what six people in a converted children's ward had been appointed to do.

The query went out on the standard form, three lines, requesting an
itemised breakdown of the excess against the established schedule, and it
went out to Hollernbruch the way every query went out, addressed to the
establishment rather than to a name, because the desk had never in eight
years learned a single name at that site and saw no reason March should be
different, and the desk did not know, entering the reference number, that
the movement in question was a single subject under armed escort, carried
under conditions the schedule had no line for, and would not have known
even if the file had said so, because the file said nothing of the kind —
it said transport, one consignment, special handling — and special
handling was itself an ordinary phrase, appearing on perhaps forty
requisitions a year, most of them concerning glassware.

Hollernbruch's reply came back within the week, itemised as requested: an
armed escort of four, drawn from the garrison at a supplementary rate; a
sealed rail car requisitioned outside the normal freight allocation; two
nights' subsistence for the escort at a location the reply did not name.
The desk read the breakdown, found each figure individually defensible and
the total nonetheless twelve hundred per cent above the established norm
for a consignment the file listed as weighing under two hundred pounds,
and released the movement anyway, because the figures reconciled to the
explanation given, and the desk's authority extended to whether a number
matched its justification, not to whether the justification was one a
reasonable person ought to have questioned — a distinction the desk had
drawn a hundred times before and drew again now, filing the correspondence
under Transport, 1943, where it has sat since, unread, as far as the desk
knows, by anyone.

The desk has queried eleven other transports since, and a proposal to
reclassify Procedure Seuche that it declined twice on grounds of adequacy,
and the March file is, to the desk, indistinguishable in kind from any of
them — a discrepancy, noted, resolved, closed. Nobody at Section VI has
asked the desk what the transport carried, this being a question no
requisition has ever required an answer to, and the desk has never asked
either, this not being, as far as the desk has had occasion to consider it,
the sort of thing the desk's authority runs to.

The ledger stays open some evenings past when the others have gone, the
March line ringed in the same red pencil used for every correction on the
page, and the desk could not say, if anyone at Hollernbruch had ever
thought to ask — and nobody has — whether the pencil came out because the
arithmetic still bothered it, or because the arithmetic had simply never,
in eight years, been permitted to stop being the last thing the desk
looked at before it went home.
