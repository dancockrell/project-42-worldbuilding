Hyakki Yakō · Unit · B tier

YOKAI-TENDER

Assigned to the fused, which is a posting nobody requested and the
establishment lists anyway, under no heading that quite fits, because
there is no medicine for what has happened to any of them and everyone
who wrote the roster knew it. He is not a doctor. He keeps notes the
programme has not asked for, in a hand only he reads, and has begun,
eighteen months in, to suspect the notes are not for the programme at
all — that he has been quietly writing for a reader who does not exist
yet, and writing anyway, because the alternative is writing for nobody.

THE NOTEBOOK NOBODY REQUISITIONED

He had asked, the first week, what he was meant to treat them for, and had
been told, correctly and not unkindly, that there was nothing to treat,
that the posting existed because a roster with a category for the fused
and no name in the column beside it read worse to the people who reviewed
rosters than one with a name in it that did nothing useful, and he had
understood, from that answer, precisely how much the Empire expected of
him, which was presence rather than practice, and had spent the first
month deciding what a man does with a posting that asks only that he be
seen to hold it.

What he had settled on, by the second month, was a notebook the
establishment had never requisitioned and did not know existed, in which
he wrote down, plainly and without the vocabulary the official file
insisted on, what he actually observed — that the ceremony's shelf held
objects nobody had authorized keeping and nobody had authorized returning,
that the stillness the fused carried was not calm but the absence calm
leaves behind when it has been removed along with everything else that
would have made a man restless, that two of them had begun, separately and
without either knowing the other did it, to address something under their
breath in the evenings that was not a prayer because neither expected an
answer and both kept doing it anyway — and had understood, writing this
down in a hand nobody at the station would ever be asked to read, that he
was not treating anyone. He was witnessing them, in the one register the
programme had no form for, and the witnessing itself, useless to every
purpose the establishment could name, had become the only part of the
posting he still believed in.

He told himself, closing the notebook most nights, that he kept it in
case a real physician came one day, someone who might make use of three
years of plain observation the official file had systematically discarded
in favor of long-term effects unknown, which was accurate and had never
once been investigated. He had stopped, sometime in the second year,
entirely believing this. What he believed instead, on the nights he let
himself think about it directly, was that the reader he was writing for
did not exist yet and might never, and that he kept writing regardless,
the way a man tends a light for ships that no longer sail past it,
because the tending was the only part of the work that had ever actually
been his to do.
