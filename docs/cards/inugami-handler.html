INUGAMI-HANDLER

Handles what the folklore says should be handling him — the file assumes
one direction and the relationship runs in both, which nobody who wrote
the file has ever gone to the kennels to notice. He gave something up for
this, and the record does not say what. Loyalty is the word the report
uses, about a thing that did not consent to the arrangement any more than
he did. They understand each other, and neither of them chose to. He has
asked for a transfer three times and given a different reason each time.

WHAT THE FILE CALLS LOYALTY

The report described the bond the standard way reports described anything
they had no honest vocabulary for, which was to reach for the nearest word
that made the arrangement sound like it had been arrived at by two willing
parties, and loyalty did that work efficiently — a soldier loyal to his
post, a dog loyal to its handler, the whole structure legible at a glance
to anyone reading it in an office three hundred miles from the kennels —
and he had read that word about himself exactly once, early on, and had
understood immediately, the way you understand a lie told about you that
happens to be flattering, that agreeing with it would cost him nothing and
correcting it would cost him a conversation nobody at the depot wanted to
have.

What the report did not say, because nobody had asked him and he had
never once been given a form with a box for it, was what he had actually
surrendered to end up standing at that wire every evening — not
volunteered, not exactly conscripted either, but selected in the quiet,
administrative way the programme selected everyone, a decision made
somewhere above him and delivered to him afterward as a fact rather than
a request — and he had learned, in his first month at the kennels, that
whatever had been done to the animal on the other side of the wire had
been done to it in exactly the same manner: chosen for, not consulted,
handed a bond it had not asked for and expected, by the same paperwork
that governed him, to perform gratitude for it.

They did not speak, in any sense a report could have captured, and yet he
had come, over eleven months, to recognize a particular stillness in it
that meant something different from ordinary alertness, a way of holding
its weight that he had first mistaken for obedience and had come slowly to
understand as something closer to waiting — the same waiting he did
himself, most evenings, for orders that would explain what any of this
had been for — and on the nights he crouched at the wire without a task
to give it, saying nothing, wanting nothing from it, he had noticed it
settle in a way it never settled for anyone else at the depot, and had
allowed himself, just once, the thought that this might be the one honest
transaction available to either of them: not loyalty, not command, but two
things that had not chosen this, choosing, for an hour at dusk, not to be
alone in it.

He had put in for a transfer three times since, and given a different
reason each time to three different officers, none of them true, because
the true reason — that leaving would mean explaining what he was leaving,
and there was no form for that either — was not one he had found, in
eleven months, any way to write down.
