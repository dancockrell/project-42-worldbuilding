RADIOMAN SECOND CLASS UEDA SANAE

Radioman Second Class, twenty-two, headphones on when it happened, and hers
is the only account of the four that describes a sound instead of a look: a
carrier tone on no frequency the set could receive, arriving before, stopping
after. The programme tested it harder than any of the other ten theories
built to explain her and found nothing, and the account is still in the
cabinet three years later, unwithdrawn. She has stopped saying so out loud.
She has not stopped listening for it, on duty and off, at every set she has
ever been near, in case it comes back before anyone asks her again.

THE FREQUENCY THE SET CANNOT RECEIVE

By 0300 the hut holds only the sounds a hut makes when nobody is asking
anything of it: the stove ticking as it gives up its last heat, the sea doing
what the sea does against rock it has done it against for longer than the
station has stood there, and under both of those, so far under that Ueda has
stopped being able to say whether she hears it or has simply decided to,
the set's own hiss, which is not silence and has never once in three years
been silence, though everyone who is not on watch believes that it is.

She logs the hour, the frequency, the nothing. That is the whole of the
job at 0300, and she has done it so many times that her hand writes *0300,
14.7, clear* while the rest of her goes on listening the way you go on
walking after your legs have stopped waiting for instructions.

Ogawa's light sweeps the ceiling every eleven seconds, the way it has swept
every ceiling at the station since before any of them arrived, and she has
learned to log against it without meaning to, one entry per two sweeps, so
that the paper itself keeps a kind of time nobody asked it to keep.

At 0311 there is a tone.

She would not call it a tone to anyone who asked, because a tone is a thing
with a source, and a source is a thing a set can be tuned toward and away
from, and this is neither. It sits under 14.7 the way the hiss sits under
silence, present in a place the dial says nothing is, and she has three
years of practice now at not moving when it happens, at keeping the pencil
where the pencil was, at breathing the number of times a person breathes
who has heard nothing worth remarking on.

She could write it down. There is a line on the form for *anomalous
signal, characteristics*, put there for exactly this, by somebody who has
never once had reason to fill it in on her watch, because she has never
once filled it in, because the last time she said the words out loud to a
lieutenant with a clipboard he wrote *not on any frequency the set could
receive* in a hand that made it sound like a fault in her hearing rather
than a fault in the world, and ten better-argued theories died faster
than that sentence did, and this one is hers, and she has decided, without
ever deciding it in a sentence she would sign, that a thing tested this
hard and found this thoroughly nowhere belongs to the person who found it
and not to a file two men who were not present will write about it in
November.

It does not come again. At 0313 the hiss is only the hiss.

She writes 0311, 14.7, clear, which is true, in every sense the form
was built to hold, and turns the log a quarter turn on the desk so that
Kurosawa, coming on at four, will find it already facing him, the way she
always leaves it, the way a person leaves a room for someone else to walk
into without having to first find the light.
