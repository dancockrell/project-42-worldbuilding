TANUKI-TRICKSTER

The one genuinely funny fusion in the entire establishment, and the
programme finds this embarrassing in a way it has no form for. Filed
under an operational category invented specifically to contain him,
because no existing one applied. Nothing he does is regulation. Everything
he does works. Amatsu has twice declined, in writing, to discipline him,
and has not stated why either time. He is the only fused man at the
station who talks about what happened to him openly, at volume, usually
as a joke, and nobody has told him to stop.

THE ONLY MAN WHO SAYS IT OUT LOUD

He had discovered, in the first month, the one fact about the station
that every other fused soldier had discovered and then quietly buried:
that nobody in a position to punish him for it actually wanted to be the
officer who wrote up a formal reprimand reading *subject persists in
making light of his own selection*, because the sentence, set down in an
official hand and filed where a colonel might eventually read it, sounded
worse for the establishment than for him, and he had understood this
instinct correctly and early, and had spent every day since building on
it with the confidence of a man who has found a door nobody else
noticed was unlocked.

So he told it as a story, at the long table after supper, the way other
men told stories about girls back home or fish that had gotten away — the
morning something reached into a rice paddy outside Nagano and decided,
for reasons it had never troubled to share with the farmer's son standing
in it, that this particular unremarkable young man belonged to the
Empire's folklore now, and how the young man in question had gone home
that evening and told his mother nothing, because what precisely would he
have said, and how he had shown up at the induction depot a week later
still not entirely sure he hadn't imagined the whole thing, right up
until the officiant at the ceremony had said a word over him that made
the hair on his arms stand up in a way no word had ever managed before or
since — and the room laughed, every time, at the parts that were
genuinely funny, and went quiet, every time, at the one line he always
delivered flat and fast and moved past before anyone could ask him to
say more about it, which was that he had never once gotten an answer for
why him, and had stopped expecting one somewhere around the second
telling.

Amatsu had heard the story, or a version close enough, from an aide who
had reported it up with some anxiety, expecting a reprimand to follow, and
had read the report twice and written nothing back, and when asked
directly, once, by a junior officer wanting cover for his own inaction,
had said only that a man laughing about what had been done to him was not
the same thing as a man forgetting it, and that she would rather have one
soldier who said the truth loudly at supper than four hundred who never
said it at all — which was not, strictly, an order, and had nonetheless
traveled through the establishment faster than any order she had ever
actually given, and had left him exactly where he already was: telling
the story his own way, at his own volume, to a table that kept laughing
in the right places and going quiet, right on schedule, at the one place
that was never actually a joke.
